Let’s get Started

What do you know about George Lucas?

Stage 1 - Lead-in (8 min, Ss-Ss). Display image of George Lucas. Elicit what Ss know: films (Star Wars, Indiana Jones), characters, technology, influence. Build a word cloud on the board. Then transition: 'Now look at these sentences about George Lucas. Something is wrong with each one.'

What’s wrong with these sentences?

1 George Lucas make movies about space. makes
2 The fans of Star Wars is very loyal. are
3 There is six Star Wars films in the original series. are
4 Each of the films have a unique story. has
5 Many Thai students loves Star Wars. love
6 A lot of the special effects is computer-made. are
Reveal corrections one by one. Elicit why each word changed. Don't explain rules yet — just build awareness.

Diagnostic Test

Each sentence has ONE error.

1. George Lucas have changed the film industry. has "George Lucas" is singular
2. There is two main characters in Star Wars. are Subject is "characters" (plural)
3. Each of the Star Wars movies have a different director. has "Each" is always singular

Let’s Learn the Rules

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 1

Rule 1: Ignore prepositional phrases

“The color of her eyes changes.”

Stage 3 - Teach Clarifying (12 min, T-Ss). For each rule: return to diagnostic test items that show the problem, then display marker sentence. Elicit the rule from students before confirming.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 1

Rule 1: Ignore prepositional phrases

“The color of her eyes changes.”

The subject is color → changes (singular). The noun inside the prepositional phrase (eyes) does NOT control the verb.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 2

Rule 2: There + be — the subject follows

There are several kinds of flowers.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 2

Rule 2: There + be — the subject follows

There are several kinds of flowers.

Look ahead! kinds is the real subject → are (plural). There is never the subject.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 3

Rule 3: Each, one, neither, either are always singular

Each of the films has a unique story.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 3

Rule 3: Each, one, neither, either are always singular

Each of the films has a unique story.

Each is always singular → has. The noun after of (films) does NOT control the verb.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 4

Rule 4: Both, several, many are always plural

Many Thai students love Star Wars.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 4

Rule 4: Both, several, many are always plural

Many Thai students love Star Wars.

Many, both, several always take a plural noun → plural verb. Don’t let the determiner trick you — check the noun that follows it.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 5

Rule 5: Some, all, most, a lot, none — check the noun

A lot of the special effects are computer-made.

A lot of my time is spent in the library.

Subject-Verb Agreement: Rule 5

Rule 5: Some, all, most, a lot, none — check the noun

A lot of the special effects are computer-made. (countable plural)

A lot of my time is spent in the library. (uncountable singular)

Check the noun after of. If it’s countable plural → use are/were. If it’s uncountable singular → use is/was.
