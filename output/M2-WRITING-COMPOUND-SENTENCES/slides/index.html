How to Build a Compound Sentence

Simple vs. Compound

Simple: one subject, one verb (or compound verb)

My family goes camping every summer and usually has fun.

Compound: two subjects, two verbs, comma + coordinator

My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun.

"Elicit: Which sentence has two subjects? What makes the second one compound?"

Simple vs. Compound

Simple: one subject, one verb (or compound verb)

My family goes camping every summer and usually has fun.

Compound: two subjects, two verbs, comma + coordinator

My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun.

Coordinator: BUT

Meaning: connects two opposite ideas

"She likes classical music, but she doesn't like rock."

"The period became comma+but. The capital 'S' became lowercase — one sentence now, showing contrast."

Coordinator: OR

Meaning: shows a choice or alternative

"Every Friday, they go to a concert. They visit an art gallery."

Option 1 + or + Option 2

"Two separate activities. How do we show they're choices?"

Coordinator: OR

Meaning: shows a choice or alternative

"Every Friday, they go to a concert, or they visit an art gallery."

Option 1 + or + Option 2

"The period became comma+or. The capital 'T' became lowercase — one sentence showing a choice."

Coordinator: SO

Meaning: shows a reason and its result

"They both like jazz. They go to jazz concerts together."

Reason + so + Result

"Two sentences. One is the reason, one is the result. How do we show that?"

Coordinator: SO

Meaning: shows a reason and its result

"They both like jazz, so they go to jazz concerts together."

Reason + so + Result

"The period became comma+so. The capital 'T' became lowercase — cause and effect in one sentence."

The Comma Rule

Comma BEFORE the coordinator

"My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun."

S V   , coordinator S V

"The comma goes before AND/BUT/OR/SO, not after!"

Simple or Compound?

Practice 8 Strategy

Step 1: Find the subject S

Step 2: Find the verb V (just check — how many S-V pairs?)

Step 3: Does it have TWO S-V pairs + comma + coordinator?

Yes → Compound (SV, and SV). Add a comma.

No → Simple (SV, SVV, or SSV). No comma.

Choose the Right Coordinator

Practice 9 — items 1 to 13

Fill in: and, but, or, or so.

Rapid oral check. Call out item numbers, students shout the answer. Then display answers with reasons.

Practice 9 — Answers (1–3)

1. and joins two nouns "...fried chicken and meatloaf."
2. and joins two clauses (compound) "I ordered meatloaf, and my friend ordered fried chicken."
3. or after negative (not ... or) "We don't have chicken or meatloaf."
Item 3: 'don't' is negative → use 'or' instead of 'and'.

Practice 9 — Answers (4–6)

4. but contrast — opposite ideas "I wanted to leave immediately, but my friend wanted to stay."
5. and / but two blanks — nouns + contrast "He ordered a hamburger and french fries, but I didn't order anything."
6. so result — reason → result "My new neighbors are vegetarians, so they don't eat meat."
Item 5: first 'and' joins two nouns in a simple sentence. Second 'but' contrasts the two clauses.

Practice 9 — Answers (7–9)

7. or / but two blanks — negative + contrast "They don't eat meat or chicken, but sometimes they eat a little fish."
8. so result — reason → result "I wanted to be friendly, so I invited them to my house for dinner."
9. and compound verb (simple sentence) "They came and brought their young son." No comma — compound verb
Item 7: 'don't eat' → 'or' not 'and'. Then 'but' adds contrast. Item 9: 'came AND brought' = compound verb, simple sentence. No comma.

Practice 9 — Answers (10–12)

10. so result — reason → result "He is just a baby, so he can't talk yet."
11. or / so two blanks — negative + result "They don't drink coffee or tea, so I served lemonade."
12. or choice — alternative "...a choice of chocolate cake or apple pie."
Item 11: 'don't drink' → 'or' not 'and'. Then 'so' shows the result.

Practice 9 — Answer (13)

13. but contrast — opposite ideas "The husband wanted both cake and pie, but the wife didn't want either."
BUT shows contrast between the husband's desire and the wife's lack of interest.
