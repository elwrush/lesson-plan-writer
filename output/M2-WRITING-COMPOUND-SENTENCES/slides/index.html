How to Build a Compound Sentence

Simple vs. Compound

Simple: one subject, one verb (or compound verb)

My family goes camping every summer and usually has fun.

Compound: two subjects, two verbs, comma + coordinator

My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun.

"Elicit: Which sentence has two subjects? What makes the second one compound?"

Simple vs. Compound

Simple: one subject, one verb (or compound verb)

My family goes camping every summer and usually has fun.

Compound: two subjects, two verbs, comma + coordinator

My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun.

Coordinator: AND

Meaning: connects two similar ideas

"My roommate is an art student. Her boyfriend plays in a rock band."

Meaning: connects two opposite ideas

Coordinator: BUT

Meaning: connects two opposite ideas

"She likes classical music, but she doesn't like rock."

Idea 1 + but + opposite idea

"The period became comma+but. The capital 'S' became lowercase — one sentence now, showing contrast."

Coordinator: OR

Meaning: shows a choice or alternative

"Every Friday, they go to a concert. They visit an art gallery."

Option 1 + or + Option 2

"Two separate activities. How do we show they're choices?"

Coordinator: OR

Meaning: shows a choice or alternative

"Every Friday, they go to a concert, or they visit an art gallery."

Option 1 + or + Option 2

"The period became comma+or. The capital 'T' became lowercase — one sentence showing a choice."

Coordinator: SO

Meaning: shows a reason and its result

"They both like jazz. They go to jazz concerts together."

Reason + so + Result

"Two sentences. One is the reason, one is the result. How do we show that?"

Coordinator: SO

Meaning: shows a reason and its result

"They both like jazz, so they go to jazz concerts together."

Reason + so + Result

"The period became comma+so. The capital 'T' became lowercase — cause and effect in one sentence."

The Comma Rule

Comma BEFORE the coordinator

"My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun."

S V   , coordinator S V

"The comma goes before AND/BUT/OR/SO, not after!"

Simple or Compound?

Practice 8 Strategy

Step 1: Find the subject S

Step 2: Find the verb V (just check — how many S-V pairs?)

Step 3: Does it have TWO S-V pairs + comma + coordinator?

Yes → Compound (SV, and SV). Add a comma.

No → Simple (SV, SVV, or SSV). No comma.

compound

Compound with 'or'. Second clause: 'we sat and told' — compound verb within a compound sentence.
