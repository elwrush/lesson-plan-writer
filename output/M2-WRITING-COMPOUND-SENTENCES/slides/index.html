How to Build a Compound Sentence

Simple vs Compound

Simple (compound verb):

My family goes camping every summer and usually has fun.

Formula: S V V (one subject, two verbs)

Compound:

My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun.

Formula: S V, and S V (two subjects, two verbs)

Point to each word as you read. "Simple: one subject, two actions. Compound: two subjects, two actions, comma + and."

Coordinator: AND

Meaning: connects two similar ideas

"My roommate is an art student, and her boyfriend plays in a rock band."

Idea 1 + and + Idea 2

Elicit: "Are these two ideas similar or opposite?" (similar). "Both positive." Check understanding: "She doesn't like rock, AND he doesn't like art."

Coordinator: BUT

Meaning: connects two opposite ideas

"She likes classical music, but she doesn't like rock."

Idea 1 + but + opposite idea

"BUT shows contrast — the second idea goes the other way."

Coordinators: OR and SO

OR = choice or alternative

"Every Friday, they go to a concert, or they visit an art gallery."

SO = reason and result

"They both like jazz, so they go to jazz concerts together."

"OR gives a choice between two options. SO means the first idea causes the second."

The Comma Rule

Comma BEFORE the coordinator

"My family goes camping every summer, and we usually have fun."

S V , coordinator S V

Stress: "The comma goes before AND/BUT/OR/SO, not after!" Show on board: comma + coordinator = compound sentence.

Simple or Compound?

Practice 8 Strategy

Step 1: Underline the subject S

Step 2: Underline the verb V V

Step 3: Is there a coordinator joining two subjects?

Yes → Compound (SV, and SV). Add a comma!

No → Simple (SV, SVV, or SSV). No comma.

Choose the Right Coordinator

Practice 9 — Answers (reference)

1. and   2. and   3. or   4. but   5. and/but   6. so   7. or/but

8. so   9. and   10. so   11. or/so   12. or   13. but

Key rule: In negative sentences, use or not and (items 3, 7, 11)

Item 9: "came AND brought" — compound verb, simple sentence. No comma needed. Item 5: first blank joins nouns, second joins clauses.

Practice 10A — Model Answers (1–5)

1. Canada has two languages, so everything is in English and French.
2. There are hundreds of languages, but not all have a written form.
3. Chinese is spoken by more people, but English is spoken in more countries.
4. Russian is the third most spoken, and Spanish is the fourth.
5. There are one million words in English, but most people use only ten thousand.
Alternatives for 2 and 3: "and" is also acceptable. Emphasize: comma + coordinator rule is the same regardless of which coordinator.

Practice 10A — Model Answers (6–9)

6. Chinese has many dialects, so Chinese people cannot always understand each other.
7. French used to be for diplomacy, but now it is English.
8. International companies are growing, so they will need more bilingual workers.
9. Young people should know a second language, or they will be at a disadvantage.
Item 7: BUT shows contrast (past vs present). Item 9: OR shows consequence (if you don't → disadvantage).
