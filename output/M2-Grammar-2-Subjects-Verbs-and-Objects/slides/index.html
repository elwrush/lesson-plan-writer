Stage 2 · Controlled Practice · 12 min · S, Ss-Ss, T-Ss Aim: To give learners structured practice identifying subjects, verbs, and objects. Procedure: - Demo item 1 together first. - Ss work individually (5 min). - Pair check (3 min). - Whole-class feedback: display answers. Discuss items with linking verbs (1, 5). - Items 7 (no object) and 9 (compound subject).

Practice 3 — Answers (1–3)

Subjects, Verbs, and Objects

1. S My youngest brother V is in high school. Linking verb — no object

2. S He V watches O TV and V does O homework. Two verbs, two objects

3. S He V works at a shopping mall. Prepositional phrase — no object

Item 1: linking verb "is" — no object. Item 2: two verbs, two objects (watches TV, does homework). Item 3: "at a shopping mall" is a prepositional phrase, not an object.

Practice 3 — Answers (4–6)

Subjects, Verbs, and Objects

4. S He V likes O his job but V doesn't like O his boss. Two clauses — two objects

5. S His job V is easy and V pays well. Linking verb "is" + "pays" — no object

6. This semester, S he V is taking O extra classes. Action verb — has object

Item 5: "is" is linking verb, "pays" has no object here. Item 6: "is taking" is an action verb (present continuous), NOT a linking verb. Has object "extra classes".

Practice 3 — Answers (7–9)

Subjects, Verbs, and Objects

7. S He V will go to college next year. Phrasal verb — no object

8. S He V speaks and V understands O English very well. Two verbs share one object

9. On weekends, S he and his friends V play O soccer. Compound subject

Item 7: "go to" is a phrasal verb — no object. Item 8: two verbs share one object (speaks English, understands English). Item 9: compound subject "he and his friends".

Practice 3 — Answer (10)

Subjects, Verbs, and Objects

10. S He V doesn't have O a girlfriend yet. Subject · Verb · Object

Item 10: straightforward — subject "He", verb "doesn't have", object "a girlfriend". Ask: "Any questions about Practice 3 before we move on?"

First Steps in Academic Writing, Practice 4 (p. 15)

Stage 3 · Semi-Controlled Practice · 12 min · S, Ss-Ss, T-Ss Aim: To give guided practice identifying and completing sentences missing subjects or verbs. Procedure: - Ss work individually: read the paragraph, add missing subjects or verbs (5 min). - Remind Ss: look for word groups that feel incomplete. Ask "Who?" or "What action?" - Pair check (3 min). - Display corrected paragraph. Discuss alternative answers (e.g., sentence 3: "He" vs "Larry").

Practice 4 — Answers (1–3)

Larry's Bad Habit

1. My friend Larry has a bad habit. Correct
2. He ___ never on time to anything. He is never on time — missing verb
3. ___ arrives ten minutes late everywhere. He arrives — missing subject
Item 1: correct as written. Item 2: missing verb "is" (linking verb). Item 3: missing subject "He". Could also accept "Larry".

Practice 4 — Answers (4–6)

Larry's Bad Habit

4. Larry ___ always an excuse. Larry has always an excuse — missing verb
5. "I missed the bus." Correct
6. "My alarm clock didn't ring." Correct
Item 4: missing verb "has". Could also accept "gives" or "makes". Items 5-6: correct — Larry's excuses are complete sentences.

Practice 4 — Answers (7–9)

Larry's Bad Habit

7. "My watch stopped." Correct
8. "My mother telephoned me just as I was leaving." Correct
9. He uses each excuse at least twice a week. Correct
All three are correct. Point out that the direct quotes (items 5-8) are all complete sentences — each has a subject + verb + complete thought.

Practice 4 — Answers (10–12)

Larry's Bad Habit

10. I know them all. Correct
11. Whenever Larry rushes in... I can finish the sentence. Correct
12. Larry's bad habit ___ not a problem for me any longer. habit is not a problem — missing verb
Items 10-11: correct. Item 12: missing verb "is". Could also accept "has become".

Practice 4 — Answers (13–14)

Larry's Bad Habit

13. If the movie ___ at 7:00, I tell Larry it starts at 6:45. movie is at 7:00 — missing verb
14. Then he ___ early! he is early — missing verb
Items 13-14: both missing the linking verb "is". Ask: "What kind of verb were all three missing items (12, 13, 14)?" (linking verb "is"). Notice the pattern: linking verbs are often dropped in informal speech.

First Steps in Academic Writing, Practice 5 (p. 15)

Stage 4 · Freer Practice · 14 min · S, Ss-Ss, T-Ss Aim: To give learners extended practice applying both sentence-boundary recognition and capitalization rules. Procedure: - Ss work individually: read the paragraph, find where 14 sentences begin and end (6 min). - Remind Ss: use the 6 capitalization rules from the previous grammar lesson (first word, pronoun I, names, nationalities, places). - Pair check (4 min). - Display corrected paragraph as continuous text with capitals highlighted. - Discuss tricky spots: "Anju" already capitalized, "a young couple" vs "The husband" etc.

Practice 5 — Answers (1–3)

My Neighbors

1. A young couple from India lives next door to me. R1
2. The husband's name is Ajay. R1
3. The wife's name is Anjuli. R1

R1 = capitalize first word of each sentence

All 3 needed R1. Note: "India" already capitalized (R4 — place name). "Ajay" and "Anjuli" already capitalized (R3 — people's names).

Practice 5 — Answers (4–6)

My Neighbors

4. Everyone calls her Anju. R1
5. They have a young son and are expecting their second child. R1
6. They hope to have a girl this time. R1
Items 4-6: all needed R1. "Anju" is a name (R3) — correctly capitalized in the original.

Practice 5 — Answers (7–9)

My Neighbors

7. Both Ajay and Anju have good jobs. R1
8. He is an executive in a computer company. R1
9. She is a computer programmer and works in our local hospital. R1
All 3 needed R1. "Ajay" and "Anju" are names (R3) — already capitalized.

Practice 5 — Answers (10–12)

My Neighbors

10. Anju is a wonderful cook. Already correct — name (R3)
11. She cooks mostly Indian food. R1
12. They sometimes invite neighbors on weekends for a potluck meal. R1
Item 10: "Anju" was already capitalized — no change needed because it's a name (R3). This is the only sentence in the paragraph that didn't need a capital added. Items 11-12: both needed R1.

Practice 5 — Answers (13–14)

My Neighbors

13. We all bring something to share. R1
14. It is fun to live next door to Ajay and Anju. R1

R1 = capitalize first word · 13 of 14 sentences needed R1 · Sentence 10 was already correct

Items 13-14: both needed R1. Summary: 13 of 14 sentences needed a capital added (R1). Only sentence 10 ("Anju is a wonderful cook") was already correct. Ask: "Which rule did most of these corrections use?" (R1 — first word of a sentence).
