What if you woke up in a different body every day?

Lesson: Introducing Every Day (Reading Circle) Duration: 46 minutes · Class: M3-4A · CEFR: B2 Teacher: Ed Rush Materials: Every Day by David Levithan (Chapter 1), YouTube trailer, composite cast image, reflection questions handout

Here's what you'll be able to do

  • I can understand the start of a unusual story
  • I can find important details about the narrator
  • I can share my thoughts about identity and belonging

These are skills you use when you read any story in English!

Some important words

Important Words

inhabit /ɪnˈhæbɪt/

A doesn't just visit each person — they inhabit the body for a full day, feeling everything the person would feel.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Inhabit means to live in or occupy a place or body. A doesn't just observe — they inhabit the person."

drifter /ˈdrɪftər/

A calls themself a drifter — moving from one life to the next without ever settling down.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "A drifter is someone who moves from place to place without a fixed home or purpose. A drifts through different bodies."

ephemeral /ɪˈfemərəl/

A's time in each body is ephemeral — it only lasts one day, then it's gone forever.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Ephemeral means lasting for a very short time. Each of A's identities is ephemeral."

profound /prəˈfaʊnd/

Spending the day with Rhiannon has a profound effect on A — it changes how they see the world.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Profound means very great or intense, deeply meaningful. A has never felt such a deep connection before."

transparent /trænsˈpærənt/

Rhiannon's emotions are transparent — A can see exactly how she feels, even when she tries to hide it.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Transparent usually means see-through, but for emotions it means obvious and easy to read."

Why Read Aloud?

Why do we read together?

  • It helps you read more fluently
  • You learn new words from the story
  • You hear the natural rhythm of English
  • We share the experience together
Stage 3 · 3 min · T-Ss Elicit: "Why do we read out loud together in a reading circle?" Highlight the benefits. Then explain the format: 20 min guided read-aloud → private reading with reflection questions.

Let's Read

Reading Circle — Day 5994

  • Take turns reading paragraphs aloud
  • Follow along as your classmates read
  • Think about A's voice — what kind of person is A?
Stage 4 · 20 min · T-Ss Goal: To guide learners through the opening of the novel. Procedure: - Teacher and students take turns reading aloud — each student reads 1-2 paragraphs then passes. - Pause after the first page: "What is unusual about the narrator?" (A wakes up in a different body each day) - Pause after the beach scene: "Why is A breaking the rules? What does Rhiannon represent for A?" - Draw attention to the central question: Can A ever truly connect with someone?

Your Turn to Read

Read and Reflect

  • Read the chapter on your own
  • Write your answers to the reflection questions
  • This is for you — there are no right or wrong answers
Stage 5 · 10 min · S Goal: To give learners time to absorb the text individually and respond personally. Hand out reflection questions (printed or point to screen). If time permits, invite 1-2 students to share what they wrote. Reflection questions: 1. Who (or what) is the narrator? What do we learn about A in Chapter 1? 2. Why does A decide to spend the day with Rhiannon? 3. A says: 'I am a drifter, and as lonely as that can be, it is also remarkably freeing.' What does A mean? 4. Choose one moment from the beach scene and explain why it is important. 5. At the end, A writes 'Remember that you love Rhiannon.' Why does A do this?
