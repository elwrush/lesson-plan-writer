Notes Demo 1: Key Words

Video says:

"It's Gen Z that holds the strongest traditional beliefs when it comes to gender roles with 31% of men believing a wife should obey her husband."

↓

You write:

Gen Z = strongest trad beliefs
31% men: wife obey husband

Stage aim: Demonstrate key-word note-taking. Procedure: Point to the crossed-out sentence. Say: "You don't need 'it's,' 'that,' 'the,' 'when.' You need the numbers, the comparison, the result." Then point to the notes below the arrow.

Notes Demo 2: Abbreviations

Video says:

"61% of Gen Z men and 51% of Gen Z women say they feel enough has already been done for gender equality."

↓

You write:

61% men, 51% wom → enough done for equality

Stage aim: Demonstrate abbreviations and symbols in notes. Procedure: Point out that "women" became "wom," and the arrow → replaces "say they feel." Say: "Your notes don't need full sentences. They need the data and the relationship between ideas."

Key Vocabulary

/ˈmænəsfɪə/

manosphere (noun)

After watching several manosphere videos, he started posting comments about traditional gender roles.

Word: manosphere | noun | A collection of online communities where men promote traditional gender roles. Stage aim: Pre-teach key vocabulary for listening comprehension.

/ˈekəʊ ˈtʃeɪmbəz/

echo chambers (noun)

Social media algorithms create echo chambers where your feed only shows opinions you already agree with.

Word: echo chambers | noun | Situations where people only encounter beliefs or opinions that reinforce their own.

/ˈtrædwaɪvz/

tradwives (noun)

She started following tradwife influencers who post videos about cooking and cleaning for their husbands.

Word: tradwives | noun | Short for "traditional wives" — influencers who glamorise traditional domestic lifestyles.

/mɪˈsɒdʒɪnɪst/

misogynist (noun)

The documentary interviewed a self-described misogynist who said negative things about women.

Word: misogynist | noun | A person who dislikes, despises, or is strongly prejudiced against women.

/ˈbredwɪnə/

breadwinner (noun)

In the 1950s, the father was usually the breadwinner while the mother stayed home with the children.

Word: breadwinner | noun | The person who earns money to support a family.

/səbˈmɪsɪv/

submissive (adjective)

The man on the TV show said he wanted a submissive wife who would let him make all the decisions.

Word: submissive | adjective | Ready to conform to the authority of others; meekly obedient or passive.

Part 1 — Question 1

What did the Kings College study find about Gen Z's beliefs on gender roles compared to older generations?

Gen Z holds the strongest traditional beliefs. 31% of men say a wife should obey. 33% say husband has final word. "It's Gen Z that hold the strongest traditional beliefs" — Para 1
Discuss the answer. Play the embedded clip to replay the evidence at 1:18-1:47.

Part 1 — Question 2

What percentage of Gen Z men think a husband should have the final word on important decisions?

33% of Gen Z men "33% of men... a husband should have the final word" — Para 1
Discuss. Play clip at 1:43-1:47.

Part 1 — Question 3

How do Gen Z women's views compare to Gen Z men's on these questions? Give one example.

Women agree much less. 18% vs 31% on obedience. 19% vs 33% on final word. "31% of men and 18% of women believing a wife should always obey her husband" — Para 1

Part 3 — Question 1

What is a "tradwife" and what content do these influencers create?

Part 3 — Question 2

What did the University of Melbourne study find about adolescent boys' attitudes?

Adolescent boys most likely to support violence against feminism. 40% of boys 13-17 say women lie about violence. "Support for violence to resist feminism was highest amongst adolescent boys... around 40% agreed" — Para 3
Discuss the study findings. Play clip at 6:47-7:15.

Let's Discuss

Introducing Your Point of View

"I think that … because …"

"In my opinion, …"

"It seems to me that …"

"One piece of evidence that supports this is …"

Stage aim: To introduce functional language for introducing a point of view. Timing: 2 min. Procedure: Read through the phrases. Model one with a volunteer: "I think that Gen Z holds traditional beliefs because 33% of men think a husband should have the final word."

Acknowledging Someone Else's View

"That's an interesting point. I'd add that …"

"I see what you mean. However, …"

"I hadn't thought of it that way. …"

"You make a good point about … but have you considered …?"

Stage aim: To introduce functional language for acknowledging and responding to others' views. Timing: 2 min. Procedure: Model a short exchange with a volunteer using phrases from both slides. Then tell students: "Now you'll practice these in pairs."

Think-Pair-Share

Think — Question 1

To what extent do you agree that Gen Z holds the strongest traditional beliefs about gender roles? Use at least two pieces of evidence from the video.

Suggested structure

1. State your position: "I (agree / partly agree / disagree) that …"

2. Evidence 1: "The video showed that …"

3. Evidence 2: "Another finding was that …"

4. Conclude: "Overall, this suggests that …"

Timer: 2 minutes. Then discuss with your partner.

Stage aim: To apply evidence from the listening to a critical discussion question using scaffolding. Timing: 5 min total (2 min think + 2 min pair + 1 min share). Procedure: Start the timer. Students write notes individually. After 2 min, tell students to pair up and discuss using the techniques from the previous slides. After 2 min, elicit 2-3 responses.

Think — Question 2

How can young people critically evaluate the social media content they see about gender roles?

No set structure — respond in your own way.

Timer: 2 minutes. Then share with your partner.

Stage aim: To apply critical thinking to a discussion question without scaffolding. Timing: 5 min total (2 min think + 2 min pair + 1 min share). Procedure: Start the timer. Students write notes. After 2 min, pairs discuss. After 2 min, elicit 2-3 responses. Provide content and language feedback.
