Let's Learn

What Makes a Complete Sentence?

1. A sentence must have a subject -- who or what the sentence is about

"He speaks three languages."

Stage 4 -- Teach Clarifying (5 min, T-Ss). Display first criterion. Read the sentence. Ask: "Who speaks three languages?" (He).

What Makes a Complete Sentence?

1. A sentence must have a subject -- who or what the sentence is about

"He speaks three languages." = subject

2. A sentence must have a verb -- what the subject does or is

"He speaks three languages."

'He' turns yellow with underline — students see WHERE the subject is. Now ask: "What does He do?" (speaks).

What Makes a Complete Sentence?

1. A sentence must have a subject -- who or what the sentence is about

"He speaks three languages." = subject

2. A sentence must have a verb -- what the subject does or is

"He speaks three languages." = verb

3. A sentence must express a complete thought -- the reader is not left asking "and then?"

"He speaks three languages." Complete

"They didn't like" ...like what?

'Speaks' turns yellow. Now show check/cross — the sentence with a complete thought gets a check, the fragment gets a cross. Transition to fragments.

Why Is This NOT a Sentence?

Type Problem Fix
No subject Is very hot today. It is very hot today.
No verb The children hungry. The children are hungry.
Incomplete thought They didn't like. They didn't like the movie.

Every fragment is missing something. The fix adds the missing part.

Walk through each row. Ask: "What's missing?" before revealing (clicking to next slide is enough). For incomplete thought: hardest one — has subject and verb, but the thought isn't finished.

The Exception: Command Sentences

Command sentences — the subject is understood

"Hurry!" → (You) hurry!

"Don't be late." → (You) don't be late.

"Go to bed!" → (You) go to bed!

Display command sentences. Ask: "Who hurries? Who doesn't be late?" Elicit: the subject is YOU — it's understood.

The Exception: Command Sentences

Command sentences — the subject is understood

"Hurry!" → (You) hurry!

"Don't be late." → (You) don't be late.

"Go to bed!" → (You) go to bed!

Commands are still complete sentences — they express a complete thought. The verb is always in the simple (dictionary) form.

All three '(You)' turn yellow simultaneously. Emphasize: the subject is YOU in every command. Quick check: Ss identify commands from p. 11.

Capitalization Rules

Capitalization Rules 1-3

RuleExample
1. First word in a sentence"My neighbor is a mechanic."
2. The pronoun I"My friends and I study together."
3. Names of people and their titles
Not a title without a name: "He's a king."		"President Putin" · "Mrs. Simpson"

RuleExample
