From Testing to Learning

You've seen where the problems are.

Now let's learn the rules — what makes a real sentence?

Stage 4 · 10 min · T-Ss Transition from diagnostic to teaching phase. Signal shift: "You've tested yourselves. Now I'm going to show you exactly how to fix these problems."

What is a sentence?

A sentence must have:

a subject who or what does the action "Is very hot today." — No subject! ✗
a verb the action or state "The children hungry." — No verb! ✗
a complete thought the idea is finished "Because it's raining." — Not a complete thought! ✗
✓ "He speaks three languages fluently." — Subject + verb + complete thought = SENTENCE!
Stage 4 · 10 min · T-Ss Aim: To clarify the sentence definition, targeting only the areas the diagnostics exposed as weak Use 2-3 examples from Test 1 to illustrate each criterion.

One Exception — Commands

The subject is always you (understood).

Commands: Be quiet.   Wait for me!   Speak slowly.

Negative: Don't eat so much!   Don't worry.

Stage 4 · 10 min total · T-Ss Introduce command sentences as the exception (subject 'you' is understood).

From Commands to Capitals

Now let's look at the six rules for capital letters.

Stage 4 · 10 min · T-Ss Transition from commands to capitalization rules. Signal shift: "Next — the other big problem from your writing. Let's learn the six rules."

Capital Letters — Six Rules (1–3)

R1 First word in a sentence My neighbor is a mechanic.
R2 The pronoun I My friends and I often study together.
R3 Names of people and their titles President Putin / Professor Jones
BUT: a president / a professor

Capital Letters — Six Rules (4–6)

R4 Nationalities, languages, religions, ethnic groups Swedish, English, Muslim, Asian
R5 School courses with numbers History 101 / Physics 352
BUT: history / math / physics
R6 Specific places on a map South America, Lake Titicaca, New York City
Stage 4 · 10 min · T-Ss Display the six rules on screen one at a time. For each rule, reference the correct answer from Test 2 as an example. Spend extra time on rules students missed most in the diagnostic. Quick oral check: "What do we capitalize?"
