Sentence Types 2 B2

Learn to spot and fix incomplete sentences

Here's what you'll be able to do

  • I can decide if a sentence is complete or incomplete
  • I can find what is missing — the subject or the verb
  • I can correct fragments by adding the missing part

Let's Learn

What Makes a Complete Sentence?

Subject + Verb = Complete Sentence

"My best friend always helps me with my homework."

Subject: My best friend   |   Verb: helps

Return to diagnostic test. Use show of hands for hardest items. Elicit: "What must every English sentence have?" Board the rule. 4 min.

Fragment Type 1: Missing Subject

The sentence has a verb, but no subject. Who or what is doing the action?

is very kind and generous.

Fragment — no subject

Fragment Type 1: Missing Subject

Add a subject to fix it.

She is very kind and generous.

Complete sentence — subject added

Fragment Type 2: Missing Verb

The sentence has a subject, but no verb. What is the subject doing or being?

My friend always there for me.

Fragment — no verb

Fragment Type 2: Missing Verb

Add a verb to fix it.

My friend is always there for me.

Complete sentence — verb added

One Exception

Commands like "Stop!" or "Listen carefully."

The subject you is implied — these are NOT fragments.

Briefly note the exception. Students copy the two fragment types and the exception into notebooks. 2 min.
