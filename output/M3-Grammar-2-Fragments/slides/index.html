Can You Spot the Problem?

Why Fragments Are a Problem

Fragments make your writing confusing for the reader.

With Fragments

Maria loves her cat. Feeds it every morning. Her cat always hungry.

Corrected

Maria loves her cat. She feeds it every morning. Her cat is always hungry.

Two fragments: missing subject (Feeds)  |  missing verb (always hungry)

Show this comparison slide. Elicit: "Which text is clearer? Why?" Point out: "Feeds it every morning" — who feeds? Missing subject. "Her cat always hungry" — what is the cat doing/being? Missing verb. Emphasise: fragments confuse the reader. Complete sentences make your meaning clear. 2 min.

Let's Learn

What Makes a Complete Sentence?

Subject + Verb = Complete Sentence

"My best friend always helps me with my homework."

Subject: My best friend   |   Verb: helps

Return to diagnostic test. Use show of hands for hardest items. Elicit: "What must every English sentence have?" Board the rule. 4 min.

Fragment Type 1: Missing Subject

The sentence has a verb, but no subject. Who or what is doing the action?

is very kind and generous.

Fragment — no subject

Stage 3 — Teach (12 min, T-Ss). Return to diagnostic test. Use show of hands for hardest items. Introduce Fragment Type 1: "This sentence has a verb but no subject — who or what is doing the action?" Click to reveal the fix.

Fragment Type 1: Missing Subject

Add a subject to fix it.

She is very kind and generous.

Complete sentence — subject added

The green slot and subject "She" appear together via auto-animate. Emphasise: "The subject tells us WHO does the action. Without it, the sentence feels incomplete." Students copy the example.

Fragment Type 2: Missing Verb

The sentence has a subject, but no verb. What is the subject doing or being?

My friend always there for me.

Fragment — no verb

Introduce Fragment Type 2: "This sentence has a subject, but no verb — what is the subject DOING or BEING?" Click to reveal the fix.

Fragment Type 2: Missing Verb

Add a verb to fix it.

My friend is always there for me.

Complete sentence — verb added

The green slot and verb "is" appear together via auto-animate. Emphasise: "The verb tells us what the subject DOES or IS. Without it, the sentence is incomplete." Students copy the example.

One Exception

Commands like "Stop!" or "Listen carefully."

The subject you is implied — these are NOT fragments.

Briefly note the exception. Students copy the two fragment types and the exception into notebooks. 2 min.
