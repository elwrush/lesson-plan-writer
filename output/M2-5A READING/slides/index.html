Reading: Receptive Skills

Today's Goals

1. I can find the main idea of a text about food.

2. I can find specific details in a reading text.

3. I can share my ideas and opinions about what I read.

Stage aim: To orient students to the lesson outcomes. Timing: 1 min. Interaction: T-Ss. Procedure: Welcome Ss. Read each outcome aloud. Ask Ss to put up fingers: 1 finger = easy, 3 fingers = hard for each goal. This gives you a quick sense of how confident they feel about today's skills.

True or False? Or Not Given?

True, False, or Not Given?

We'll try one together from Exercise D.

"Very few people now work in farming in Indonesia."

Step 1: Read each part carefully. What is being claimed?

The sentence claims that not many people work in farming now.

True, False, or Not Given?

"Very few people now work in farming in Indonesia."

Step 1: Read each part carefully.

Not many people work in farming.

Step 2: Find the key words. Which paragraph talks about them?

farming, people, work → Look in paragraph 1.

True, False, or Not Given?

"Very few people now work in farming in Indonesia."

Step 1: Read each part carefully.

Step 2: Find the key words.

Step 3: Compare with the text. What does paragraph 1 say?

Statement: "Very few people work in farming"
Text: "A large percentage of the population works in the farming industry."

"Very few" ≠ "A large percentage" → The text says the opposite.

True, False, or Not Given?

"Very few people now work in farming in Indonesia."

Step 1: Read each part carefully.

Step 2: Find the key words.

Step 3: Compare with the text.

Step 4: Decide. Is it T, F, or NG?

Answer: FALSE

The text says "A large percentage" — that's the opposite of "very few." When the text says the opposite → False.

Remember: NOT GIVEN = the text doesn't talk about it at all. FALSE = the text says the opposite.

Emphasize the NG vs FALSE distinction. For NG to apply, the information must be absent from the entire text. Now say: "Now you try Exercise D. Read each sentence. Follow the same steps: 1) Read carefully, 2) Find keywords, 3) Compare with text, 4) Decide. You have 14 minutes."

Exercise D — Close Reading

Read the text again. Write T (True), F (False), or NG (Not Given) for each sentence.

Remember: Underline the evidence in the text for each answer.

Stage 4 · 14 min · Ss-Ss Aim: To extract specific information and distinguish between stated, false, and unstated information. 10 min individual work. Then 4 min pair check. Encourage Ss to underline the text evidence for each answer. After the timer, do class feedback with justification for each answer.

Exercise D — Answers (1–3)

1. Very few people work in farming. False Text: "A large percentage of the population works in the farming industry." The text says the opposite.
2. Indonesia does not produce any meat. Not Given Text mentions dairy and meat are imported (para 4), but doesn't say whether any meat IS produced in Indonesia.
3. Herbs and spices are new in Indonesian cooking. False Text: "Indonesia has a long history of cooking with herbs and spices" (para 3). The opposite of "new."
Check answers row by row. For each, ask: "Which paragraph did you find the evidence in?" Emphasize the difference between statement 2 (NG) and statement 3 (F). For NG: "Could not find the answer anywhere in the text" vs False: "Found the opposite."

Exercise D — Answers (4–5)

4. People now eat more processed foods. True Text: "the amount of dairy, meat, and sugar people eat has grown" and "supermarkets mostly sell processed foods" (para 4).
5. Indonesia produces all the rice and sugar it needs. False Text: "In the past, produced enough rice and sugar... but now it needs to import these foods" (para 5). Opposite of what the statement says.
Check final 2 answers. Review the TRUE/FALSE/NG rules one more time. Quick recap: "What's the difference between False and Not Given?"

Let's Discuss

How to Discuss What You Read

Step 1: Think — What did the text say?

"The text says that Indonesian people eat more processed food now."

Start with one fact from the reading.

How to Discuss What You Read

Step 1: Think — What did the text say?

"Indonesian people eat more processed food now."

Step 2: Connect — Is it the same in your country?

"In my country, we also eat more fast food than before."

Use: "In my country..." or "It's the same / different because..."

How to Discuss What You Read

Step 1: Think.

Step 2: Connect to your country.

Step 3: Explain — Why do you think that?

"I think this happens because people have less time to cook."

Use: "I think... because..." or "One reason is..."

Stage 5 · 10 min · Ss-Ss Aim: To discuss ideas from the reading and relate them to personal experience. Walk through all 4 steps using Exercise E1 as the model: "The text says Indonesians eat more dairy, meat, and sugar now. In Thailand, we also eat more fast food than before. I think this happens because people are busier and don't have time to cook. What do you think?" Then Ss discuss in small groups: 6 min discussion, 4 min feedback.

Exercise E — Critical Thinking

E1:

What changes in diet have there been in Indonesia? Have you had similar changes in your country?

E2:
