Key Vocabulary

Important Words

/kənˈteɪdʒəs/

contagious (adjective)

The flu is very contagious. When one person gets it, many others get sick too.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Check: "If a disease is contagious, can it spread?" Yes.

/ˈæntɪˌbɒdiz/

antibodies (noun)

When you get a vaccine, your body makes antibodies. These special proteins help fight the disease.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Connect to Part 1: "Serum therapy used antibodies from animals."

/ˈaʊtbreɪk/

outbreak (noun)

There was an outbreak of food poisoning at school. Twenty students got sick from the same meal.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Connect to Part 2: "There's a diphtheria outbreak in Australia."

/bækˈtɪəriə/

bacteria (noun)

Bacteria are tiny living things. Some bacteria can make you sick, but others are helpful.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). "Diphtheria is caused by toxin-producing bacteria."

/ˈbuːstə/

booster (noun)

Children get a booster shot a few years after their first vaccine to make the protection stronger.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). "11-to-13-year-olds can get a booster in Australia."
