Three Comma Rules

Rule 1: Time Order Signals

Put a comma after most time order signals at the beginning of a sentence.

Yesterday, I did homework for three hours.

Finally, I was too tired to think.

Exception: Then, Soon, and Now usually do NOT take a comma.

Then I went home. (no comma)

Rule 1 from textbook p.33. Emphasise the exception: then, soon, now do NOT take a comma. Use ERRANT examples from M3-3A feedback where students added comma after "then."

Rule 2: Compound Sentences

Put a comma BEFORE the coordinator in a compound sentence.

I was too tired to think, so I decided to watch TV.

Simple sentences with compound verbs: NO comma.

I woke up and finished my homework. (one subject — no comma)

Rule 2 from textbook p.33. The distinction between compound sentence (two S-V pairs, needs comma) and compound verb (one S, two Vs, no comma) is the key teaching point.

Rule 3: Items in a Series

Put a comma between items in a series of three or more.

I bought apples, bananas, and oranges.

Only two items? NO comma needed.

Red and gold are the school colors. (two items — no comma)

Rule 3 from textbook p.33. The Oxford comma (comma before 'and' in a series) is used in this textbook. Two items never take a comma — this is a common error.

Which Comma Rule to Use?

Question 1: Is there a time signal at the start? → Rule 1

Question 2: Two S-V pairs with and/but/or/so? → Rule 2

Question 3: Three or more items in a list? → Rule 3

Some sentences need more than one rule (e.g., Rule 1 + Rule 2)

Model items 1 and 4 from 3A as examples. Item 1 needs Rule 3 (series). Item 4 needs Rule 1 + Rule 2 (time signal + compound).

Freewriting

What is Freewriting?

Write without stopping for a set time. Don't worry about errors — you can fix them later.

Rules:

1. Keep your pencil moving — don't stop.

2. Don't correct errors — fix them in the next draft.

3. Try to use compound sentences with correct commas.

Explain: freewriting is Step 1 of the writing process. Read the model on the next slide aloud. Then start the 10-minute timer.

Freewriting: A Memorable Event

Write without stopping for 10 minutes about a memorable event in your life.

Keep writing — don't stop to correct. Try to use compound sentences with correct commas.

10 minute timer. Students freewrite on the topic. After: optional share one sentence with a partner. This freewrite is Step 1 of the writing process for the CA paragraph.
