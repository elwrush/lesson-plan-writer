Three Comma Rules

Three Comma Rules

Rule 1: After a time order signal

"Yesterday, I did homework for three hours."

Rule 2: Before a coordinator in compound sentences

"I was tired, so I took a break."

Rule 3: Between items in a series (3+)

"I got up, took a shower, and ran out the door."

"Three rules today. Each one is about joining things — time + sentence, sentence + sentence, item + item."

Three Comma Rules

Rule 1: After a time order signal

"Yesterday, I did homework for three hours."

Exception: Then, soon, now — NO comma.

Rule 2: Before a coordinator in compound sentences

"I was tired, so I took a break."

Only for compound sentences — NOT simple sentences!

Rule 3: Between items in a series (3+)

"I got up, took a shower, and ran out the door."

Only for 3+ items — NOT for 2 items.

"The exception on each rule is important. Then/soon/now = no comma. Simple sentences = no comma. Two items = no comma."

When NOT to Use a Comma

"My mom was okay with it too so, I can play piano."

Wrong! Comma goes BEFORE so, not after. Correct: "...too, so I can play piano."

"They were happy and they were poor."

Wrong! Compound sentence needs a comma before 'and.'

"Tomiko and Keiko are from Japan."

Correct! Simple sentence with compound subject. NO comma before 'and.'

"He went to the store and bought milk."

Correct! Simple sentence with compound verb. NO comma.

Show real ERRANT errors: M3-3A students put commas after 'so' instead of before it. Teach the difference: simple (no comma) vs compound (comma).

Practice: Add the Commas

How to Add Commas

Step 1: Check for a time signal at the start.

Add comma after it (unless it's Then/Soon/Now).

Step 2: Check for two S-V pairs + coordinator.

Compound sentence? Add comma before the coordinator.

Step 3: Check for a list of 3+ items.

Add commas between them. Comma before 'and' too.

Let's Freewrite!

What is Freewriting?

You write without stopping for a set time.

Don't worry about mistakes — just keep writing.

If you get stuck, write "I don't know what to write" until a new idea comes.

Model (from p.34):

"I'm supposed to freewrite for ten minutes about a memorable event in my life. I don't know what to write about. Maybe about my brother's boat accident. We were so scared..."

Explain: "Your topic is 'A Memorable Event.' This will be your CA paragraph topic. Freewrite for 10 minutes."

My Memorable Event

Freewrite for 10 minutes without stopping.

Try to use compound sentences (and, but, or, so).

Don't worry about commas — you can fix them later!
