# Reading — Author's Viewpoint {#reading-task}

<i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i> **Standard** — Open your book to p. 39. Read the article and answer Exercise 2 with the questions visible.

<i class="fa-solid fa-pencil"></i> **Advanced** — Read the article first without looking at the questions. Take notes on the author's viewpoint. Then answer.

::: notes
Time: 4 min. Interaction: Indiv → Pairs → T-Ss. Students choose their level. Exercise 2: identify the author's viewpoint for each of the 4 opinion pairs. Then pair check, whole-class feedback. Elicit which parts of the text confirm each answer.
:::

# Q1 — Powerful Pictures {#answer-ex2-q1}

**Author's viewpoint:** [a — Pictures can be very powerful]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Reason:** [The author dedicates the opening paragraphs to listing photography's powers — transport, emotional reach, propaganda, manipulation — but introduces doubt with *"are we still under their spell?"* The power is real but not absolute.]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Evidence:** [*"A photograph has the power to transport us to places where we have never been… to reach into our hearts and minds… to deceive us and make us buy things."*]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

::: notes
Click three times: answer → reason → evidence. Ask: "Which sentence shows the author is not sure about the power anymore?" (Line 25: "are we still under their spell?")
:::

# Q2 — Too Many Pictures {#answer-ex2-q2}

**Author's viewpoint:** [b — We see too many pictures]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Reason:** [The author cites the figure of two billion pictures posted daily and says this flood makes pictures feel more stressful than wonderful. The quantity itself is the problem.]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Evidence:** [*"Approximately two billion pictures are posted every day… When we are drowning in a flood of pictures, they can become more stressful than wonderful."*]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

::: notes
Click three times. Point to the paragraph: "What number does the author use to prove there are too many pictures?" (Two billion daily.)
:::

# Q3 — Snapshots or Serious? {#answer-ex2-q3}

**Author's viewpoint:** [a — We prefer snapshots to serious photography]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Reason:** [The author describes most daily images as *"everyday snapshots"* — selfies, cats, families — and dismisses them as *"nice, but not powerful."* This shows the author thinks our attention is on disposable content rather than meaningful photography.]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Evidence:** [*"Most of the pictures that we see on a daily basis aren't all that wonderful anyway. They are mostly everyday snapshots … Nice, but not powerful."*]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

::: notes
Click three times. Elicit: "What examples does the author give of everyday snapshots?" (Selfies, cats, families, friends, celebrities with new babies.)
:::

# Q4 — Media Overload {#answer-ex2-q4}

**Author's viewpoint:** [b — Images affect us less due to the media]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Reason:** [The author gives two statistics — 3,500 marketing messages a day and 99% making no impact — to prove that media over-saturation has made us immune to images.]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

**Evidence:** [*"It has been estimated that some people see as many as 3,500 marketing messages a day… 99% of those images make little or no impact on us. We are immune to their magic."*]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

::: notes
Click three times. Contrast with Q1: photos CAN be powerful, but the media delivers so many that most have lost their effect.
:::
