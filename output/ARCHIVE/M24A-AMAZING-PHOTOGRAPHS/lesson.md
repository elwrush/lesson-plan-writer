= Lesson Stages

## Stage 1: Lead-in — Afghan Girl

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss → Pairs

**Aim:** To activate interest in the power of photographs through a famous, prize-winning image

- Display the Afghan Girl photograph on screen without giving any context
- Ask: "What do you notice about this girl? How does she make you feel?"
- Elicit a few responses, then reveal: "This photograph won every major prize in photography. It was taken by Steve McCurry in 1984 in a refugee camp in Pakistan."
- Ask pairs to discuss: "Why do you think this photo is so famous? What makes it powerful?"
- Brief whole-class feedback, then transition: "Today we're going to read about whether pictures still have power over us."

## Stage 2: Opinions about Photography

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** Indiv → Pairs → T-Ss

**Aim:** To activate personal opinions about photography before reading

- Students open their books to p. 38. Read the four opinion pairs in Exercise 1 and choose the option that best matches their view
- In pairs, students compare their choices and explain why
- Whole-class feedback: take a quick poll for each pair (show of hands for a vs b) and discuss any strong disagreements
- Read the *Reading strategy* box aloud: "Identifying an author's viewpoint"
- Check comprehension: "What does *impartial* mean? Why does it matter when we read?"

## Stage 3: Pre-teach Vocabulary

**Time:** 4 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To remove blocking vocabulary from the reading text so students can process the article more fluently

- Display each word on screen with its example sentence from the article (fragment reveal with audio where available):
  - *deceive* — "the pictures in propaganda and advertising have the power to deceive us and make us buy things"
  - *manipulate* — "Pictures can manipulate us because we believe what we see"
  - *propaganda* — "the pictures in propaganda and advertising have the power to deceive us"
  - *bombardment* — "the constant bombardment of images in the news and advertising"
  - *immune* — "99% of those images make little or no impact on us. We are immune to their magic."
- Check comprehension with concept questions for each word:
  - *deceive*: If someone deceives you, do they tell the truth or a lie?
  - *manipulate*: When you manipulate someone, are you controlling them or helping them?
  - *propaganda*: Is propaganda usually designed to inform you or influence you?
  - *bombardment*: If you are bombarded with messages, do you get a few or a lot?
  - *immune*: If you are immune to an advert, does it affect you or not?

## Stage 4: Reading for Viewpoint and Detail

**Time:** 13 min  |  **Interaction:** Indiv → Pairs → T-Ss

**Aim:** To identify the author's viewpoint and find specific information in the article

- **Exercise 2:** Students read the article "Overexposure" on p. 39 and identify the author's viewpoint for each of the four opinion pairs from Exercise 1
  - Pair check, then whole-class feedback
  - Elicit which parts of the text confirm each viewpoint
- **Exercise 3:** Play audio 1.10. Students read and listen, then answer the six comprehension questions individually
  - Encourage students to underline the part of the text where they found each answer
  - Pair check, then whole-class feedback with text evidence
  - Discuss sidebar questions as a class: "What does the author mean by 'images at our fingertips'? Why does the author use the word 'bombardment'?"

## Stage 5: Vocabulary — Compound Adjectives

**Time:** 9 min  |  **Interaction:** Indiv → Pairs → T-Ss

**Aim:** To understand and practise compound adjectives formed from a noun and a present participle

- Read the *Discover vocabulary* box on p. 38. Explain the pattern: noun + present participle = compound adjective (e.g., *heart* + *breaking* = *heartbreaking*)
- **Exercise 4:** Students find the six compound adjectives in the article and match each one to a definition 1–6
  - Pair check, then feedback. Drill pronunciation: *breathtaking, eye-popping, heartbreaking, mind-numbing, neverending, thought-provoking*
  - Concept check: "Which adjective means very boring? Which means interesting?"
- **Exercise 5:** Students rewrite four sentences by creating a compound adjective from the highlighted words
  - Review the example: *a dish that makes my mouth water → a mouth-watering dish*
  - Students work individually, then pair check
  - Whole-class feedback on the board

## Stage 6: Discussion — Talking Points

**Time:** 7 min  |  **Interaction:** Groups → T-Ss

**Aim:** To discuss and respond to the ideas in the article using personal opinions

- Display Exercise 6 questions on screen:
  1. *Do you agree with the author's viewpoint? Why? / Why not?*
  2. *Do you think we take too many pictures? Why? / Why not?*
- Students think individually for 1 minute, noting down ideas
- In small groups (3–4), students discuss both questions, using compound adjectives where possible
- Groups share one interesting point from their discussion with the class
- Teacher rounds off by returning to the Afghan Girl photo: "Do you remember this picture for more than a second?"

## Stage 7: Wrap-up

**Time:** 3 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To consolidate key vocabulary and reflect on the lesson

- Quick vocab recall: T says a definition, students call out the compound adjective (e.g., "very sad" → *heartbreaking*, "visually stimulating" → *eye-popping*)
- Ask 2–3 students: "What is one thing you'll remember from today's lesson?"
- Preview: Next lesson — Discover grammar on p. 119
