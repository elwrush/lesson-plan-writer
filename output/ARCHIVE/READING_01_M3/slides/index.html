Introducing The Outsiders B2

Explore the beginning of a classic story

Lesson: Introducing The Outsiders (Reading Circle) Duration: 46 minutes · Class: M3 · CEFR: B2 Teacher: Ed Rush Materials: The Outsiders by S.E. Hinton (Chapter 1), YouTube trailer, composite cast image, reflection questions handout

Here's what you'll be able to do

  • I can understand the start of a story
  • I can find important details about the characters
  • I can share my thoughts about what I read

These are the same skills you use when you read in English every day!

Read through each outcome. Elicit: "Which of these sounds the easiest? The hardest?" Connect to prior knowledge: "You already do these things when you read in Thai." Explain: "Today we start a new novel together — The Outsiders."

What does it mean to be an outsider?

  • Think about a time you felt like an outsider.
  • Was being an outsider different in the 1960s than it is today?
Pair discussion: "What does it mean to be an outsider? Can you think of a time you felt like one?" 3 min pair discussion. Then 2 min class feedback. Bridge to the novel: "The Outsiders is a story about teenagers who feel like outsiders — and how they find belonging."

Some important words

Important Words

/ˈɡriːsər/

greaser

The greasers slick their hair back and wear leather jackets — they're the tough kids from the poor side of town.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Elicit: "In the story, greasers are the poor kids from the East Side. They have long, greased-back hair."

/ˈsoʊʃɪz/

Socs

The Socs drive nice cars and live on the West Side — they have money and cool clothes.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Note: "Socs" is short for "Socials". They are the rich kids from the West Side — the rivals of the greasers.

/ˈrekən/

reckon

Ponyboy looked at the dark clouds. "I reckon it'll rain," he said, pulling his jacket tighter.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Reckon means 'think' or 'guess'. It's informal — people use it in casual conversation."

/ˈsævi/

savvy

Johnny didn't savvy what Dally meant, so he asked him to explain.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Savvy means 'understand' or 'get it'. It's slang — Two-Bit uses it a lot."

/ˈsuːpt ʌp/

souped-up

Two-Bit's souped-up car was so loud you could hear it coming from a street away.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Souped-up means a car that has been changed to go faster. It has a more powerful engine."

Why Read Aloud?

Why do we read together?

  • It helps you read more fluently
  • You learn new words from the story
  • You hear the natural rhythm of English
  • We share the experience together
Stage 3 · 3 min · T-Ss Elicit: "Why do we read out loud together in a reading circle?" Highlight the benefits. Then explain the format: 20 min guided read-aloud → private reading with reflection questions. Emphasize: "You don't need to be perfect — just try your best."

Let's Read

Reading Circle — Chapter 1

  • Take turns reading paragraphs aloud
  • Follow along as your classmates read
  • Think about the characters and the setting
Stage 4 · 20 min · T-Ss Goal: To guide learners through the opening of the novel. Procedure: - Teacher and students take turns reading aloud — each student reads 1-2 paragraphs then passes. - Pause after the Soc attack scene: "What just happened to Ponyboy? How is he feeling?" - As each character is introduced (Darry, Sodapop, Steve, Two-Bit, Dally, Johnny), pause to note key traits. - Draw attention to Ponyboy's narrative voice.

Your Turn to Read

Read and Reflect

  • Read the chapter on your own
  • Write your answers to the reflection questions
  • This is for you — there are no right or wrong answers
Stage 5 · 10 min · S Goal: To give learners time to absorb the text individually and respond personally. Hand out reflection questions (printed or point to screen). If time permits, invite 1-2 students to share what they wrote. Reflection questions: 1. Who is telling the story? What do we learn about the narrator in Chapter 1? 2. What happened to Ponyboy after the movie? How did the gang help him? 3. What are the Greasers and the Socs? How are they different? 4. Choose one character and describe them in 2-3 sentences. 5. Ponyboy says: "I lie to myself all the time. But I never believe me." What does this tell us about him?
