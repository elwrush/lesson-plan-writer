Introducing A Monster Calls B1

The monster showed up just after midnight. As they do.

Lesson: Introducing A Monster Calls (Reading Circle) Duration: 46 minutes · Class: M2 · CEFR: B1 Teacher: Ed Rush Materials: A Monster Calls by Patrick Ness (Chapters 1 & 2), YouTube trailer, composite film image, author photo, reflection questions handout

Here's what you'll be able to do

  • I can understand the opening of a story about monsters and truth
  • I can find important clues about Conor's hidden fear
  • I can share my thoughts about the monster's purpose

These are skills you use when you read any story in English!

Read through each outcome. Elicit: "Which of these sounds the easiest? The hardest?" Connect to prior knowledge: "Today we start a new novel together — A Monster Calls."

A Monster Calls — Trailer

What do you think this story is about? What kind of monster do you expect?

Play the trailer (~2 min). After it ends, elicit initial reactions. Stage 1 · 8 min total (incl. discussion on next slide) Goal: To activate learners' interest and build anticipation. Ask: "What do you think this story is about? What kind of monster do you expect?"

What would you do if a monster came to your window at midnight?

  • Would you be scared? Or curious?
  • What kind of monster would frighten you the most?
Pair discussion: "What would you do if a monster came to your window at midnight?" 3 min pair discussion. Then 2 min class feedback. Bridge to the novel: "A Monster Calls is about a boy named Conor who is visited by a monster — a yew tree that walks and talks. But this monster wants something more frightening than Conor expects. Today we read Chapter 1 together."

Author Spotlight

About the Author

  • Patrick Ness — born 1971 in the US, moved to the UK
  • A Monster Calls won the Carnegie Medal and the Kate Greenaway Medal — the only book ever to win both
  • Ness won the Carnegie two years in a row — only the 2nd author ever
  • Over 1 million copies sold worldwide, translated into 30+ languages
Stage 2 · 5 min · T-Ss Goal: To give learners context about the author and the novel's unique origin. Key points: Siobhan Dowd wrote four brilliant YA novels. This would have been her fifth. Ness won the Carnegie Medal for this book, and Jim Kay won the Kate Greenaway Medal for the illustrations. Awards: British Children's Book of the Year, Red House Children's Book Award, Kitschies Red Tentacle. Sales: Over 1 million copies. Film starred Liam Neeson, Felicity Jones, Sigourney Weaver. Then transition to vocabulary pre-teach.

Some important words

/juː/

yew

The churchyard is full of yew trees, some over 2,000 years old.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "A yew is an evergreen tree with poisonous berries, often found in church graveyards. Yew trees can live for thousands of years."

/ˈʃɪmərɪŋ/

shimmering

The desert heat made the road ahead shimmering like water.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Shimmering means shining with a flickering, unsteady light — like heat haze on a road."

/ˈeɪnʃənt/

ancient

The ancient tree must now be at least 500 years old.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Ancient means extremely old, from a long time ago."

/ʌnˈteɪmd/

untamed

The wild horse had never been ridden — it was completely untamed.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Untamed means wild, not controlled or trained."

/ˈkɒmpɒst/

compost

We recycle vegetable peelings and grass cuttings to make compost for the garden.

Drill: teacher says → class repeats (×3). Explain: "Compost is decayed organic material used to feed plants."

Why Read Aloud?

Why do we read together?

  • It helps you read more fluently
  • You learn new words from the story
  • You hear the natural rhythm of English
  • We share the experience together
Stage 3 · 3 min · T-Ss Elicit: "Why do we read out loud together in a reading circle?" Highlight the benefits. Then explain the format: 20 min guided read-aloud → private reading with reflection questions.

Let's Read

Reading Circle — Chapters 1 & 2

  • Take turns reading paragraphs aloud
  • Follow along as your classmates read
  • Think about Conor — why isn't he afraid of the monster?
Stage 4 · 20 min · T-Ss Goal: To guide learners through Chapters 1 & 2 of the novel. Procedure: - Teacher and students take turns reading aloud — each student reads 1-2 paragraphs then passes. - Pause after the opening lines: "What is effective about this opening?" - Pause after 'He'd told no one about the nightmare': "Why do you think Conor keeps the nightmare a secret?" - Pause when Conor says 'Not of you, anyway': "Why isn't Conor afraid of the monster?" - Discuss the final line of Ch1: "You will be. Before the end." What does the monster mean? - Continue into Ch2 (BREAKFAST). Pause after Conor cleans up yew leaves: "Why is Conor hiding the evidence?" - Pause after 'I wish you didn't have to be quite so good': "What does this tell us about Conor's life?"

Your Turn to Read

Read and Reflect

  • Read Chapters 1 & 2 on your own
  • Write your answers to the reflection questions
  • This is for you — there are no right or wrong answers
Stage 5 · 10 min · S Goal: To give learners time to absorb the text individually and respond personally. Hand out booklet with reflection questions (printed). If time permits, invite 1-2 students to share what they wrote. Reflection questions: 1. Conor hides the yew leaves in the bin and lies to his mother about hearing him last night. Why do you think he keeps the monster a secret? What would you do if a monster visited you? 2. Conor's mother says 'I wish you didn't have to be quite so good.' What does this tell us about Conor's life at home? How has his life changed since his mother became ill?

What you can do now
