Reading for Gist

Reading for Gist or Detail

Reading for Gist

• Find the general idea

• Don't focus on every word

• Use titles, headings & images

• Look for content words

Reading for Detail

• Find specific information

• Read slowly and carefully

• Read parts more than once

• Check you haven't missed anything

Finding Details

True or False Strategy

"The term 'super recognizer' was first used by Richard Russell in 2009."

Step 1: What does the statement claim?

  • The term "super recognizer" existed before 2009
  • Richard Russell was the person who first used it
  • It was first used in 2009

True or False Strategy

"The term 'super recognizer' was first used by Richard Russell in 2009."

Step 2: Find the evidence in the article

"The term 'super recognizer' was first used in an article by American psychologist Richard Russell and his team in 2009."

— Paragraph 2

True or False Strategy

"The term 'super recognizer' was first used by Richard Russell in 2009."

Step 3: Compare and decide

The article says Richard Russell first used the term in 2009. All three claims match.

TRUE

Rule: If all claims match the text TRUE. If even one claim doesn't match FALSE.

Stage aim: To model the T/F decision-making process before students attempt the exercise. Time: 3 min. Interaction: T-Ss. Procedure: Walk through each step. Elicit why the answer is TRUE. Emphasise the rule: all claims must match.

Answering Comprehension Questions

"Can anybody become a super recogniser?"

Step 1: Identify the keywords

Look for: anybody + become + super recogniser

These words tell you what information to search for in the article.

Answering Comprehension Questions

"Can anybody become a super recogniser?"

Step 2: Find the evidence

"Researchers believe that less than two per cent of the population have this extraordinary skill. They think it is the opposite of 'prosopagnosia' … super recognizers may inherit their remarkable ability from their parents rather than learning or developing it."

— Paragraph 2

The keywords "become" and "super recogniser" appear in this paragraph. The text says it is inherited, not learned.

Answering Comprehension Questions

"Can anybody become a super recogniser?"

Step 3: Formulate your answer

Use keywords from the question + information from the text.

No, you cannot choose to become a super recogniser. It is a natural ability that you inherit from your parents, and less than 2% of the population has it.

Rule: Use phrases from the question to start your answer. Support it with evidence from the text.

Stage aim: To model the comprehension question answering process before students attempt the exercise. Time: 3 min. Interaction: T-Ss. Procedure: Walk through each step. Elicit what keywords students identify. Show the evidence. Model the answer formulation.

Structuring Your Opinion

1. Opinion

I think I have a good memory for names because…

In my opinion, remembering faces is important because…

2. Reason

I usually remember a person's name after meeting them once.

It helps me build better relationships with people.

3. Detail / Example

Last month, I met a new colleague and remembered her name the next day.

For example, once I saw a neighbour at the supermarket and said hello immediately.
