# Lesson Stages

## Stage 1: Lead-in — BTN Video

**Time:** 10 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss → Indiv → Pair → T-Ss

**Aim:** To introduce the topic of colonisation and activate students' existing knowledge through a news video

- Show the 3 gist questions on screen (not on handout):
  - What was the Walk for Reconciliation? (When? Who? How many people?)
  - What events led up to the bridge walk?
  - Why is reconciliation still needed today?
- Read through each question, clarify that these are gist questions — main ideas only
- Play the BTN video "Bridge Walk Anniversary" (2 min 27 sec) once
- Students write brief notes individually while watching
- Pairs compare notes and agree on answers
- Whole-class feedback: elicit answers for each gist question and build a shared understanding on the board
  - Q1: 28 May 2000, Sydney Harbour Bridge, 250,000+ people, Indigenous + non-Indigenous, for reconciliation
  - Q2: Council for Aboriginal Reconciliation (1991), Redfern Speech (1992), Mabo decision (1992), Native Title Act (1993), Stolen Generations inquiry
  - Q3: Ongoing fight for recognition, equality, and justice — "still a long way to go"
- Transition: "Colonisation is still being talked about today — partly because of figures like Christopher Columbus. Let's look at the man who started it all."

## Stage 2: Columbus Elicitation

**Time:** 3 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To activate prior knowledge about Christopher Columbus before reading

- Show the Columbus image on screen
- Elicit: Who is this man? What is he famous for?
- Board key ideas: Italian explorer, 1492, "discovered" America
- Briefly acknowledge the controversy: "Some people call him a hero. Others call him a villain. Today we're going to look a little deeper."

## Stage 3: Pre-teach Vocabulary

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss → Indiv

**Aim:** To ensure understanding of five key words needed for the reading text

- Present 5 target words on screen with example sentences:

  | Word | Example sentence |
  |------|-----------------|
  | **navigator** | *Columbus was a skilled navigator who could read the stars.* |
  | **colonisation** | *The colonisation of Australia began in 1788.* |
  | **enslaved** | *The text says Columbus enslaved people who fought against him.* |
  | **immoral** | *Most people today agree that slavery is immoral.* |
  | **ruthless** | *A ruthless leader will do anything to get what they want.* |

- Check comprehension with concept questions:
  - *navigator*: Does a navigator drive a car or guide a ship?
  - *colonisation*: Is colonisation about settling in a new place or visiting it?
  - *enslaved*: Is an enslaved person free or not?
  - *immoral*: Is helping someone immoral? What about hurting someone?
  - *ruthless*: Does a ruthless person care about others' feelings?
- Students write the words in their notebooks
- Quick drill: say each word, students repeat chorally

## Stage 4: Reading — Exercises 1–3

**Time:** 10 min  |  **Interaction:** Indiv → Pair → T-Ss

**Aim:** To read for main ideas and identify each writer's opinion about Columbus

- Students open their textbooks to p. 38–39
- **Ex 1:** Read the introduction and answer in pairs:
  - Who was Christopher Columbus? What is he famous for?
  - Why might people disagree about his reputation?
- **Ex 2:** Read the two comments (Anna Craig + Guillermo Delgado) and identify each writer's overall opinion of Columbus
  - Students highlight words/phrases that show the writer's attitude
  - Pair check, then whole-class feedback
- **Ex 3:** Answer the comprehension questions
  - Students work individually, referring back to the text
  - Pair check, then whole-class feedback with text evidence
  - Key answers:
    1. Craig builds up Columbus by describing his difficult childhood and determination (para 1)
    2. She says slavery is "clearly immoral" but was accepted in the 15th century — Columbus was "a man of his times"
    3. She says he "built a bridge between the Old and New Worlds" and started a "new modern age"
    4. Delgado says Native Americans and Leif Erikson (500 years earlier) discovered the Americas first
    5. He says Columbus was motivated by economics — wealth and power, not exploration

## Stage 5: Facts vs Opinions

**Time:** 8 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss → Indiv → Pair → T-Ss

**Aim:** To distinguish between fact and opinion and apply this skill to the reading text

- Pedagogical introduction: "A fact can be proven — it's true for everyone. An opinion is what someone thinks or feels — different people can disagree."
- Use a Columbus-related example to demonstrate:
  - FACT: *Columbus sailed from Spain in 1492.*
  - OPINION: *Columbus was a brave explorer.*
  - Ask: "Which can we prove by looking at a history book? Which depends on how you feel?"
- Read the Reading Strategy box (p. 38) aloud
- **Ex 4:** Students classify 8 statements as fact or opinion
  - Individually first, then pair check
  - Whole-class feedback with justification for each:
    1. a) Fact — Italian explorer (historical record); b) Opinion — "brave" is a judgement
    2. a) Fact — measurable data; b) Opinion — "most difficult" is subjective
    3. a) Fact — historical record; b) Opinion — "improve taste" is personal preference
    4. a) Opinion — "talented" is subjective; b) Fact — biographical detail
- Extension: Elicit one more fact and one more opinion about Columbus from the class

## Stage 6: Structured Opinions

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss → Indiv

**Aim:** To learn and practise a structured framework for expressing and supporting opinions

- Present the structure on screen:

  > **Opinion** → I think that Columbus was/was not a hero because…
  >
  > **Reason** → The main reason is…
  >
  > **Evidence** → For example, when/who/what/where/how/why…

- Explain that evidence can include personal stories or examples: "When you give an opinion in a discussion, you can use a story to support it — think about when it happened, who was involved, what happened, where, how, and why."
- Teacher models with a structured opinion:
  - *I think Columbus was not a hero. The main reason is that he harmed people who were already living in the Americas. For example, when he arrived in 1492, there were already millions of people there — but he enslaved them and took their land. That's not heroic — that's cruel.*
- Students write their own structured opinion about Columbus using the framework
- Volunteers share with the class (time permitting)

## Stage 7: Think-Pair-Share — Exercise 8.1

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** Indiv → Pair → T-Ss

**Aim:** To discuss and evaluate how the same person can be seen as both a hero and a villain

- Display Exercise 8, Question 1:
  - *Why can a person be a hero and a villain to different people? Think about who is telling the story and why they are telling it.*
- **Think (1 min):** Students write their own ideas silently
- **Pair (2 min):** Pairs discuss their ideas, using the structured opinion framework from Stage 6
- **Share (2 min):** Elicit 3–4 pairs to share their ideas with the class
  - Encourage students to use specific examples from the video or reading
  - Teacher rounds off: "History depends on who tells the story — and what they want you to believe."
