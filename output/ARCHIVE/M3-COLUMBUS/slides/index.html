Answer 1

Question 1 — Paragraph 1 (Craig)
Answer Craig describes Columbus’s difficult childhood and determination to build him up as a heroic figure.
Reason She tells the story of a poor boy working on boats at age ten — this makes his later success seem earned and impressive.
Evidence “Imagine coming from a poor family in 15th century Europe. You start working on boats when you are only ten years old… Life is difficult, but you’re determined to succeed, and eventually you become one of the best navigators in the world.”

Ask: Why does Craig start with his childhood? What effect does it have on the reader?

Answer 2

Question 2 — Paragraph 3 (Craig)
Answer Craig says slavery was “clearly immoral” but was accepted in the 15th century — Columbus was “a man of his times.”
Reason She doesn’t approve of slavery, but argues we should judge Columbus by his era’s standards, not modern values.
Evidence “Slavery is clearly immoral, but in the 15th century, it was an accepted part of life. Columbus was simply a man of his times.”

Ask: Do you agree with her reasoning? Can we excuse historical figures because “everyone did it”?

Answer 3

Question 3 — Paragraph 4 (Craig)
Answer Craig says Columbus built a bridge between the Old and New Worlds, starting the modern age through exchange of culture and ideas.
Reason This is the climax of her argument — Columbus is presented as a connector, not an invader.
Evidence “Columbus built a bridge between the Old and New Worlds, creating an exchange of culture and ideas. Thanks to Columbus, a new modern age began, changing the world forever.”

The “bridge” metaphor is key. Ask: Is a bridge always positive? What travels both ways?

Answer 4

Question 4 — Paragraph 2 (Delgado)
Answer Delgado says Native Americans discovered the Americas first (they already lived there), plus Leif Erikson arrived 500 years before Columbus.
Reason He challenges the word “discovery” with two counter-arguments: the existing population and the earlier European arrival.
Evidence “In 1492, there were already millions of people living there. In addition, the first European had arrived there 500 years earlier — his name was Leif Erikson.”

If Columbus didn’t discover America, what DID he do? Is “discovery” the right word?

Answer 5

Question 5 — Paragraph 3 (Delgado)
Answer Delgado says Columbus was motivated by economics — wealth and power — not by exploration.
Reason Delgado reframes the voyage as a business venture. When Columbus didn’t find gold, he turned to enslaving people for profit.
Evidence “Columbus’ voyage was about economics, not exploration. He wanted wealth and power. … Columbus found little gold or silver in the Americas, so he decided to make money another way — by ruthlessly making native people slaves.”

Does this change how you see Columbus compared to Craig’s version?

Fact or Opinion?

Phase shift from comprehension to critical analysis.

Fact vs. Opinion

Fact can be proven. It is true for everyone. FACT: Columbus sailed from Spain in 1492.
Opinion what someone thinks or feels. People can disagree. OPINION: Columbus was a brave explorer.

Click through rows. Ask after each: How do you know this is a fact/opinion?

Pair 1 — Columbus

1a. Columbus was an Italian explorer. Fact — Historical records confirm he was Italian and made the voyage in 1492.
1b. Columbus was a brave explorer. Opinion — “Brave” is a judgement. Different people might disagree.

Ask: Why is 1a a fact? Why is 1b an opinion?

Pair 2 — The Pacific

2a. The Pacific is the largest ocean. Fact — Scientifically measurable. It is the largest ocean by surface area.
2b. The Pacific is the most difficult ocean. Opinion — “Most difficult” is subjective. Depends on experience.

Ask: What makes 2a a fact? What makes 2b an opinion?

Pair 3 — Spices

3a. Columbus brought spices from the Americas. Fact — Historical records confirm he brought back new plants.
3b. Spices improve the taste of dishes. Opinion — “Improve” is a value judgement. Not everyone likes the same flavours.

Ask: Can we prove 3a? Is 3b true for everyone?

Pair 4 — Skill

4a. Columbus was a talented sailor. Opinion — “Talented” is a judgement. Others might disagree.
4b. Columbus started sailing at ten. Fact — Biographical detail from historical sources.

Ask: Why is 4b a fact? Why is 4a an opinion?

Building Strong Opinions

Phase shift from identifying opinions to forming their own.

Structured Opinions

Opinion I think Columbus was/was not a hero because…
Reason The main reason is…
Evidence For example, when / who / what / where / how / why…

Teacher model:

I think Columbus was not a hero. The main reason is that he harmed Indigenous people. When he arrived in 1492, who were already there? Millions of people. What happened? He enslaved them. Where? In the Americas. How? It destroyed their cultures.

Students write their own structured opinion. Volunteers share.

Think. Pair. Share.
