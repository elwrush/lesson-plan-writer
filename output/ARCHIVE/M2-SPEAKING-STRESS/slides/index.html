Talking About Stress

What really stresses you out?

Get ready to tell me.

Welcome students. Today we’re going to listen to a boy talk about happiness — then YOU will talk about stress. Point to the image. “What do you think this person is feeling?” Time: 2 min. T-Ss.

Today’s Objectives

I can listen for structure in a model monolog.

I can name one thing that stresses me out.

I can give two reasons and a specific example.

Read through. “By the end of today, you’ll speak for one minute about something that causes you stress.” Time: 1 min.

Stress or Happiness?

Let’s look at some pictures. In your head, decide: does this feel like stress or happiness for you?

“Look at each picture. Think about whether it feels like stress or happiness for YOU. We’ll discuss after.” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Let’s Get Ready to Speak

“We’ve looked at stress and happiness. Now let’s learn the phrases you’ll need for your monolog.” Time: 30 sec. T-Ss.

Useful Phrases

“One thing that really stresses me out is __________.”

“The first reason is that __________.”

“Another reason is that __________.”

“For example, __________.”

“Last week / Last month / Last semester __________.”

Drill each phrase chorally. “These are the phrases you’ll use to talk about what stresses you out.” Elicit one complete sentence. Time: 3 min. T-Ss.

Part 1: Name It

Write down the thing which really gives you a headache.

“Name the thing that causes you stress. Say it clearly. Then your listener knows what you’re talking about.” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Part 2: Two Reasons

Give two reasons why

Reason 1: “The first reason is that __________.”

Reason 2: “Another reason is that __________.”

“Now give two reasons. Use ‘first’ and ‘another’ — don’t use ‘second’. ‘Another’ sounds more natural.” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Let’s Listen to Jack

Jack is 15 years old, from Sydney, and he’s going to tell you what makes him happy.

You should speak like him.

Play the audio. After listening, point out that Jack uses the same structure they will use — name it, two reasons, specific example. Time: 2 min. T-Ss.

Part 3: Specific Example

Tell a story with details

Who was there?

What happened?

Where were you?

When did it happen?

Why did it feel that way?

“Finally, tell a specific story. Include who, what, where, when, and why. This makes your monolog interesting and real.” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Hear Jack’s Example

Tell a story with details

Play audio. Students follow the WH framework while listening. After: “What was Jack’s who? What? Where? When? Why?” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Jack’s Breakdown

His happiness: Playing football with friends

Reason 1: He forgets about homework and exams

Reason 2: His friends make him laugh

Click through each part. “What’s Jack’s happiness? What’s his first reason? Second reason?” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.

Jack’s Example

Who: His best mate Liam

What: Scored a really good goal from far away

Where: At the school football field

When: Last Thursday

Why: Everyone cheered, then bought drinks from the shop — he didn’t think about school at all

“These are the WH details that make the story real. Each one answers a different question.” Time: 1 min. T-Ss.
