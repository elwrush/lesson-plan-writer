## Stage 1: Lead-in

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To activate learners' interest in the topic of stress and happiness

- Display slide with two columns: "Stress" and "Happiness"
- Show images of common stressors (exams, public speaking, homework) and happiness triggers (sports, friends, music)
- Ask: "Which side do you relate to more right now? Tell your partner."
- Pairs discuss for 2 minutes
- Whole-class feedback: elicit 2-3 responses for each column
- Transition: "Today we're going to listen to someone talk about what makes him happy — then YOU will talk about what stresses you out."

## Stage 2: Useful Language

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To present and practise key phrases for the monolog structure

- Display phrases on the slide:
  - "One thing that really ... me is ..." (stresses / makes me happy)
  - "The first reason is that ..."
  - "Another reason is that ..."
  - "For example, ..."
  - "Last week / Last month / Last semester ..."
- Model pronunciation and drill chorally
- Demonstrate that the same phrases work for BOTH stress and happiness
- Elicit one example sentence for each emotion using the phrase

## Stage 3: Structure Input — The 3-Part Framework

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To present the three-part monolog framework

- Display the three parts on the slide:
  - **Part 1: Name it** — "The thing that ___ me is..."
  - **Part 2: Two reasons** — Reason 1 + Reason 2
  - **Part 3: Specific example** — Who? What? Where? When? Why?
- Explain: "This framework works for ANY topic — happy or stressful. The structure is the same."
- Check understanding: "What goes in Part 1? What goes in Part 2? What goes in Part 3?"

## Stage 4: Model Listening — Jack's Happiness Monolog

**Time:** 6 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To expose learners to a model monolog about happiness using the three-part structure

- Pre-listening: "You're going to hear Jack, a 15-year-old boy from Sydney, Australia. He's going to talk about something that makes him happy. Listen for the three parts."
- Play Jack's happiness monolog audio
- Pair check: "What is Jack's happy thing? What two reasons does he give? What is his example?"
- Whole-class feedback using the breakdown slide

## Stage 5: Structure Analysis — Jack's Breakdown

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To identify the three parts and WH details in Jack's model monolog

- Display the breakdown slide:
  - **His happiness:** Playing football with friends
  - **Reason 1:** Forgets about homework and exams
  - **Reason 2:** Friends make him laugh
  - **Example (Who? What? Where? When? Why?):** Last Thursday, school oval, Liam scored a goal, Cokes from the shop, didn't think about school
- Students tick off each part they heard
- Elicit: "Why does Jack include who, what, where, when, and why? What does that do for the listener?" (Answer: makes the story real and interesting)
- Transition: "Now you're going to use THIS SAME structure — but for stress."

## Stage 6: Preparation — Write Your Stress Outline

**Time:** 10 min  |  **Interaction:** S

**Aim:** To give learners time to prepare their own stress monolog outline using the framework

- Display the outline template on the slide:
  - **My stressor:** ______
  - **Reason 1:** ______
  - **Reason 2:** ______
  - **Example (Who? What? Where? When? Why?):** ______
- Model with a quick teacher example: "I'm going to talk about traffic. Reason 1: it makes me late. Reason 2: hot and noisy. Example: yesterday, on Rama 4 road, I sat for 45 minutes..."
- Students write their outline independently — bullet points only, no full sentences
- Teacher circulates and helps with vocabulary and ideas
- Early finishers practise saying their monolog quietly to themselves

## Stage 7: Rehearsal — Paired Practice (Day 1)

**Time:** 7 min  |  **Interaction:** Ss-Ss

**Aim:** To give learners low-stakes rehearsal before the recorded assessment

- Students work in pairs: Speaker + Listener
- Speaker delivers their 1-minute monolog from outline (not reading full sentences)
- Listener ticks off on a checklist: "Did they name the stressor? Did they give 2 reasons? Did they give a specific example (who, what, where, when, why)?"
- Swap roles
- Teacher monitors, takes notes, and encourages students to keep their outline for Day 2
- Announce: "Tomorrow you will do this one-on-one with me. I will record it. Bring your outline."

## Stage 8: Wrap-up (Day 1)

**Time:** 3 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To highlight good language use and prepare students for the recorded session

- Praise 2-3 examples of good language use heard during monitoring
- Write 1-2 common errors on the board and elicit corrections
- Remind: "Tomorrow you speak only to me, one minute, and I record it. Use the same structure: name it, two reasons, a specific story."
- Ask: "What structure did we use today? Can you use it for other topics?" (Answer: yes — name it, two reasons, specific example)

> **_DAY 2 — Individual Recorded Monolog (in class, one-on-one with teacher)_**

## Stage 9: One-on-One Recorded PET Monolog (Day 2)

**Time:** 1–2 min per student  |  **Interaction:** T-S

**Aim:** To assess each learner's ability to deliver a one-minute structured monolog about stress

- Teacher calls students one by one to a quiet corner or separate desk
- Each student sits facing the teacher (no outline reading — outline is for reference only)
- Teacher starts recording (phone / tablet)
- Prompt: "Tell me about something that causes you stress. Remember the structure: name it, two reasons, and a specific example."
- Student speaks for up to 1 minute
- Teacher does NOT interrupt or correct during the recording
- After recording: teacher gives brief verbal feedback (one strength, one thing to improve for next time)
- **Assessment criteria** (checklist):
  - [ ] Named the stressor clearly
  - [ ] Gave Reason 1
  - [ ] Gave Reason 2
  - [ ] Included a specific example (Who? / What? / Where? / When? / Why?)
  - [ ] Spoke for at least 45 seconds
