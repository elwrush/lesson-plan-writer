Model Answer: “Is Feminism Still Relevant?”

Listen to Jack, a 15-year-old from Sydney.

As we go through his monolog, identify:

  • His opinion — where does he state it?
  • His evidence — what does he use to support each point?
  • His structure — how does he move from one part to the next?

Set the listening task. Tell students: “Listen for the structure, not just the content.” Time: 1 min.

Jack’s Opinion

“I think feminism is still really important today. Some people say it’s not needed anymore because women can vote and go to school, but I disagree.”

His view: Feminism is still relevant.

B2 feature: He uses concession — acknowledges the other side before stating his position.

Ask: “What does Jack believe? How does he show he understands the other side?” Click to reveal. Time: 1 min.

Jack’s Reason 1

“First, there are still big problems like the gender pay gap. In Australia, women earn 13% less than men for the same job. That’s not fair.”

Evidence: A statistic — 13% gap in Australia.

B2 feature: He adds evaluative language — “That’s not fair” tells us his attitude, not just the fact.

Ask: “What evidence does Jack use? What word shows his opinion about the fact?” Time: 2 min.

Jack’s Reason 2

“Second, feminism is about choice. My sister wants to be a mechanic, and some people think that’s weird. Feminism means she can do whatever she wants.”

Evidence: A personal story — his sister’s career.

B2 feature: Rephrasing — “It’s not about hating men — it’s about equality.”

Ask: “How is this evidence different from Reason 1? What phrase does he use to correct a possible misunderstanding?” Time: 2 min.

Jack’s Conclusion

“So yeah, I think feminism is still relevant. We’ve made progress, but there’s still work to do. That’s my opinion.”

Structure: Opinion → Concession (progress made) → Counterpoint (still work).

B2 feature: This concession structure is a hallmark of strong B2 — he acknowledges the opposite view without weakening his own argument.

:::

Point out the concession structure: “We’ve made progress, BUT…” Time: 1 min.
