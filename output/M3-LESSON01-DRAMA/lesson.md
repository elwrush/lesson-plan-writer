## Stage 1: Lead-in -- Speaking Feedback

**Time:** 8 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss / Ss-Ss

**Aim:** To surface the problems of flat intonation, fear, and hesitation from the first speaking performances and to frame the lesson's purpose

- Return individual feedback slips from the speaking test. Give Ss 1 min to read silently.
- Elicit: "What patterns do you see in your feedback?" Board common themes -- *flat voice*, *long pauses*, *nervous laugh*, *too quiet*.
- Pair share: "Tell your partner one thing you want to improve today." 1 min.
- Bring class back. "These are all speaking problems -- but they're also *acting* problems. Today we're going to watch how professional actors solve them."

## Stage 2: Pre-watching -- Setting the Frame

**Time:** 2 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To activate schema around table reads and set a focused viewing task

- Ask: "What's a table read? Has anyone seen one?" Elicit or explain -- actors sit around a table, read the script aloud, no costumes, no sets, no cameras.
- Set task: "We're going to watch the SAME scene twice. First, the table read -- actors reading at a table, voices only. Watch their VOICES. How do they show emotion and character using ONLY their voice?"

## Stage 3: While-watching -- Table Read + Filmed Comparison

**Time:** 5 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To observe how professional actors use voice to create emotional authenticity and compare this to the final filmed performance

- Play the Breaking Bad table read -- Blood Money argument scene, Walt and Jesse (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MqfSr8Gu9zI). 1.5-2 min. Aaron Paul's emotional read.
- Then play the equivalent filmed scene from the episode (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A-Ck_2Na6s). 1.5-2 min.
- Quick pair discussion (1 min): "What was the same? What was different?"
- Whole-class feedback. Board a comparison table:

| Same (Both versions) | Different |
|---|---|
| Emotion in voice | Body language / facial expression |
| Pace and pauses | Camera angles / editing |
| Character tone | Physical performance |

## Stage 4: Post-watching -- Elicit the Principle

**Time:** 3 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To draw out the core principle -- voice creates character, and inhabiting a role reduces self-consciousness

- Elicit: "Why does Aaron Paul's table read work, even without acting? What is he doing with his voice?"
- Guide to: *He's not being 'Aaron Paul' -- he's being Jesse. The character frees him.*
- Connect back to feedback: "Your flat intonation and fear come from being YOURSELF under pressure. What if you were someone else?"
- "The actors in Breaking Bad get into character. We're going to do the same thing with The Outsiders."

## Stage 5: Pre-reading -- Meet the Characters

**Time:** 6 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss / Ss-Ss

**Aim:** To familiarize students with the characters who speak in the Chapter 2 passage and their personas

- Play the Outsiders drive-in scene from 19:08 to 23:30 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvyYEWSK1-w&t=19m08s). All six characters appear.
- Set the focus: "Watch each character. How do they speak? What does their voice tell you about who they are?"
- After the clip, elicit voice descriptors for each character and board them:
  - **Dally** (hard, aggressive, swaggering)
  - **Cherry** (confident, sharp, warm underneath)
  - **Johnny** (quiet, scared, loyal -- then finds courage)
  - **Ponyboy** (observant, thoughtful, young)
  - **Marcia** (friendly, playful, down-to-earth)
  - **Two-Bit** (wisecracking, easygoing, street-smart)
- Assign characters for the read-aloud (depending on class size, multiple Ss can share a character or rotate).

## Stage 6: While-reading -- Group Read-Aloud

**Time:** 22 min  |  **Interaction:** S (individual reading) / T-Ss

**Aim:** To read Chapter 2 aloud with character-appropriate voice, applying the awareness developed in the preceding stages

- Ss open Chapter 2 at the line: "Dally came striding back with an armful of Cokes..."
- Teacher narrates the non-dialogue sections (Ponyboy's narration), or assign a strong reader as Ponyboy.
- Each character's dialogue is read by the assigned student(s):
  - **Dally** -- lines until he stalks off
  - **Cherry** -- confrontation with Dally, conversation with Johnny, popcorn scene, Ponyboy talk
  - **Johnny** -- defending Dally, talking back to Dally, conversation with Cherry
  - **Marcia** -- inviting the boys up, age questions, Two-Bit banter
  - **Two-Bit** -- entrance, voice imitation, Dally/Shepard explanation, banter with Marcia
- Teacher calls in: "Johnny, how would HE say that? Remember -- he's scared of Dally but found courage. What does that sound like?"
- If time permits, rotate characters and re-read a short section so different Ss try a different voice.
- End by acknowledging strong moments: "That moment when Cherry threw the Coke -- we felt it because that student read it with real anger."

## Stage 7: Wrap-up (if time allows, otherwise spills to Lesson 2)

**Time:** 0 min  |  **Interaction:** T-Ss

**Aim:** To consolidate the day's learning and preview the next lesson

- Quick round: "One word -- what did you learn about using your voice today?"
- Preview: "Next lesson, you'll create your own scene from The Outsiders and perform it -- table read first, then full performance."
