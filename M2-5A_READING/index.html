Question 1 / 8

Question1. What does the passage say about the number of children Thai women have?
AnswerD — They have fewer than one child on average.
Why?The average is 0.8 children — that is less than one. Option A says "the lowest" but the text says "one of the lowest."
Text"The average woman there has just 0.8 children in her lifetime — one of the lowest in the world."
Point out: 'one of the lowest' ≠ 'the lowest'. Make sure students understand the difference.

Question 2 / 8

Question2. What is different about today's drop in birth rates compared to the past?
AnswerA — People are choosing to have fewer children on purpose.
Why?In the past, drops were caused by war, disease, or famine. This time, people are making a choice.
Text"The number of people has gone down before, but always because of war, disease, or famine. This time, it is happening by choice. That is new."
Key distinction: past drops were forced by external events. This one is voluntary.

Question 3 / 8

Question3. What is the main purpose of the passage?
AnswerC — To explain the causes and consequences of falling birth rates.
Why?The article starts with the situation, then explains causes (paras 5-6) and consequences (para 4). This is a classic explainer structure.
Text"Why does this matter? Fewer people can mean slower economic growth..." (cause) / "So why are people having fewer children?" (consequence)
Main purpose questions test the overall structure. Point out how the article is organized: situation → causes → consequences.

Question 4 / 8

Question4. What is the main point of paragraph 4?
AnswerD — Falling birth rates affect both the economy and people's happiness.
Why?The paragraph gives two types of effects: economic (slower growth, pension problems) and human (loneliness, isolation).
Text"Fewer people can mean slower economic growth. It can also make it harder to pay for healthcare and pensions... But the human cost may be even worse."
Main point of a paragraph = the topic sentence + what it says. Point out 'But' signals a second effect.

Question 5 / 8

Question5. What does the phrase 'social isolation' mean?
AnswerC — Being separated from other people in daily life.
Why?The paragraph explains that phones let you stay in your room alone. This leads to less time with others — being separated from people.
Text"Your phone offers unlimited content in your own room. This means less time with others and fewer chances to meet a partner."
Vocabulary-in-context question. Students need to infer the meaning from surrounding text, not just from the word itself.

Question 6 / 8

Question6. What is probably true about marriage and birth rates?
AnswerB — Fewer marriages are likely to lead to fewer births.
Why?The article says most people have children after marriage. So if fewer people marry, fewer children will be born.
Text"When people struggle to find love, they marry later — or not at all. And most people still have children after marriage."
Inference question. The text doesn't say it directly — students need to connect: marriage → children, so fewer marriages → fewer children.

Question 7 / 8

Question7. What does the author suggest about government payments?
AnswerA — Paying parents is not the full answer by itself.
Why?The author says payments encourage more births, but adds that community support is also needed. Money alone is not enough.
Text"Paying parents larger sums does seem to encourage more births, but no government policy has replaced what community provides."
Author's opinion question. The word 'but' signals contrast — payments aren't the complete solution.

Question 8 / 8

Question8. Fernández-Villaverde finishes the article by saying: "This is really the only thing that matters." What does he mean?
AnswerB — Thailand's falling birth rate.
Why?The expert says Thailand's situation is "the only thing that matters." The whole article is about the falling birth rate.
Text"What Thailand is facing is a warning for the whole world. As Fernández-Villaverde says... 'This is really the only thing that matters.'"
Reference question — 'What does this/these/he refer to?' Students must look back at the preceding sentences.
