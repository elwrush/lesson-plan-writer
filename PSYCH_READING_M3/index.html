B2 Answers (1/10)

Question1. What does the passage suggest about the health effects of doomscrolling?
AnswerD. Mood and fulfilment tend to drop the more people scroll.
ExplanationThe research review found that people who doomscroll feel less well and less satisfied with their lives. The other options mix up different ideas, like the effect on work.
Transcript“Doomscrolling is linked to worse mental well-being and lower life satisfaction.”

B2 Answers (2/10)

Question2. What does the passage suggest about the August 2024 study of 800 adults?
AnswerA. It found that constant scrolling deepens fears about life itself.
ExplanationThe study found that doomscrolling raises existential anxiety, which the passage describes as dread about the limits of our existence. The other options mix up the three studies.
Transcript“The habit raises existential anxiety — the dread or panic that surfaces when we confront the limits of our existence.”

B2 Answers (3/10)

Question3. What is the main idea of paragraph 6?
AnswerD. Research keeps finding that doomscrolling harms mental health.
ExplanationParagraph 6 is about the research on doomscrolling and what it shows. The other options mention details from the paragraph, but not its central claim.
Transcript“The research picture is clear.”

B2 Answers (4/10)

Question4. Why does the writer quote the doctors?
AnswerB. To explain why people feel pushed to keep scrolling.
ExplanationThe doctors' words explain the reasons behind the habit, such as stress and the urge to keep scrolling. The statistics only show that doomscrolling and poor well-being are linked.
Transcript“Stress stokes our primary urge to scroll.”

B2 Answers (5/10)

Question5. In paragraph 7, what does the word “abstinence” mean?
AnswerC. Giving something up completely.
ExplanationThe passage says cutting back is not about abstinence but about decreasing reliance. That contrast shows that abstinence means giving something up completely.
Transcript“Cutting back is not about abstinence; it's about decreasing reliance.”

B2 Answers (6/10)

Question6. What does the passage suggest about people who have lived through violence?
AnswerB. They often keep reading bad news even as it harms them.
ExplanationThey scroll because they want to feel safe, but the passage says the constant exposure acts as a trigger and makes their anxiety worse. So they keep reading even though it harms them.
Transcript“People who have lived through violence doomscroll out of fear; they want to feel safe, so they try to follow everything that is happening.”

B2 Answers (7/10)

Question7. What does the passage suggest about the relationship between the news and the habit of doomscrolling?
AnswerA. The steady flow of distressing stories feeds the habit.
ExplanationThe media leads with bad news, and the more people scroll, the more they feel they need to. Together these ideas show that bad news keeps feeding the habit.
Transcript“The more you scroll, the more you feel you need to.”

B2 Answers (8/10)

Question8. Which statement would the writer most likely agree with?
AnswerC. People can follow world events while protecting their peace of mind.
ExplanationThe writer supports the doctors' advice that people should stay informed but protect their mental health. The passage also says stopping completely is neither realistic nor necessary.
Transcript“We need to stay informed, but not at the expense of our mental health.”

B2 Answers (9/10)

Question9. The chart shows how many adults took part in two recent studies of doomscrolling. According to the chart, which statement is correct?
AnswerA. The 2023 review drew on more adults than the 2024 study.
ExplanationThe chart shows two bars: the 2023 review drew on 1,200 adults, while the August 2024 study drew on 800. So the review involved more adults.
Transcript“A review published in April 2023 analysed three separate studies involving about 1,200 adults in total. An August 2024 study involved 800 adults.”

B2 Answers (10/10) — Summary

Question10. In no more than 50 words, summarise the writer's advice on how to reduce doomscrolling. Give one specific strategy from the passage to support your summary.
2 pointsRestates the advice (cut back rather than stop completely; build digital and social boundaries) AND names one specific strategy from the passage.
1 pointCorrect advice but no specific strategy.
0 pointsWrong claim or no passage reference.
Specific strategies students may name: keep the phone away from the bed, put it in a drawer at work, switch the screen to grayscale, turn off notifications, choose local news.
