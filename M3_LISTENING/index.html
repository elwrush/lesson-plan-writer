How hard is it to come out?
Let's watch and find out.
Greet students. Ask: What does coming out mean? Why might it be hard for a sportsperson?

Why is this lesson important?

  • You will listen to a famous song and a news interview.
  • You will hear what it means to be the first.
  • You will decide what needs to change in sport.

Entry Ticket — Cat's in the Cradle

Transition to the entry ticket. Say: We start with a famous song. The worksheet has the lyrics with gaps.
Show the picture. Ask: What might this song be about? Talk to your partner. Use Opinion-Reason-Evidence: I think... because...

Cat's in the Cradle

  • Harry Chapin wrote the song with his wife, Sandra.
  • It was his only number-one single, in 1974.
  • It sold over one million copies and went gold.
  • It is the sound of a parent’s regret — the boy grows up, and he becomes just like his dad.
Cultural impact: a father-and-son story that became iconic. Economic success: number one on the US chart and a gold record in 1974.

Main Lesson — Leigh Ryswyk

Transition to the main lesson. Say: Now we listen to a news interview with Australia's first openly gay AFL player.

Listen 1 — The big idea

Watch the video once. Don't write anything.

The video is mainly about…

AHow to become a professional AFL player.
BThe first openly gay AFL player and his decision to come out.
CHow Australian rules football began.
DFamous AFL players from the past.

Gist — Answer

AnswerB — The first openly gay AFL player and his decision to come out.
WhyLeigh Ryswyk talks about his football career and why he decided to come out publicly.
Confirm B. The interview is about Leigh Ryswyk's decision to come out as the first openly gay AFL player.

Listen 2 — Answer the Questions

Watch again. Answer the six questions on your worksheet.

Questions — Model Answers (1-3)

Check your answers.

1It was a turning point because he realised he could play well — he was best on ground for the opposition.
2He wanted to take the pressure off other people who were afraid — someone had to be the first.
3It suggests he was hiding a heavy weight and could finally be himself after coming out.

Questions — Model Answers (4-6)

Check your answers.

4No active AFL player has done it, so they do not know what to expect — the unknown is scary, and the media and fans add pressure.
5Consistent, year-round education about inclusion — not just one pride round once a year — built into workplaces and society.
6If you can be yourself, you can still achieve anything; coming out is a personal choice and is not required.

Discuss — Should Athletes Have to Come Out Publicly?

Give your opinion. Agree or disagree. Reach a conclusion.

OpinionI think athletes should not have to come out because…
AgreeI agree with you because… That's a good point.
DisagreeI disagree because… I see what you mean, but…
ConclusionSo we agree that… In conclusion, we think…

Opinion — Reason — Evidence

Timer pill: 5 minutes (300 seconds). Use the worksheet outline (opinion + three ideas) and the language table. Groups report their conclusion.
