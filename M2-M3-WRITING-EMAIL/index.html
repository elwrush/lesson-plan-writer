Most PET emails tick the boxes, but they do not flow.
Yours can do both.

Your Task

Open your worksheet. Find the email from Mrs Lake.

Look at the four notes. What do you need to write about?

Time: 2 min. Students look at their worksheet. Point out the four notes highlighted in the email.

What Must You Include?

Express enthusiasm about the party idea.

Explain your choice (classroom or park).

Suggest activities or games.

Tell Mrs Lake what food to have.

Time: 1 min. Click through each one. These are the four notes from the email.

Watch Out for Distractors

These are NOT in the notes:

Ask what time the party starts.

Say what colour decorations to buy.

Invite another teacher to join.

Time: 1 min. Click through each distractor. Ask: “Why don’t we write about these?”

Worksheet Task 2

Judge the three responses. Read them now. You have 3 minutes.

Time: 3 min. Students read the three responses from their worksheet silently. Timer counts down.

Response A

Read the full text on your worksheet.

It covers all four notes. But does it sound natural?

The party is a great idea!

I prefer the park because it has more space.

I suggest some games like football.

We should have pizza and cake.

Time: 1.5 min. Students read Response A from their worksheet. Ask: “Does it feel like a real email?”

What Went Wrong?

It covers the points, but it feels like a list.

Each sentence stands alone – no linking.

Nobody talks like this in real life.

Time: 1.5 min. Click through each weakness. Ask students how they would fix it.

Response B

Read the full text on your worksheet.

This one sounds different. What does it do well?

Thanks – I think that sounds brilliant!

I choose the park because we will have more room.

As for food, how about a bring-and-share picnic?

That way we get more variety.

Time: 1.5 min. Students read Response B from their worksheet. Ask: “What makes this one better?”

What Makes It Work?

“I think that sounds brilliant!” – real enthusiasm

“because we will have more room” – gives a reason

“As for food” – moves to a new topic smoothly

“That way” – shows the result

Time: 2 min. Click through each strength. These four features make Response B sound natural.

Response C

Read the full text on your worksheet.

This one tries. What is missing?

The party is a great idea! I am really excited.

The park would be nice. We could play games.

Everyone could bring their favourite food.

Write back soon.

Time: 1.5 min. Students read Response C from their worksheet. Ask: “What does this one miss?”

What Went Wrong?

Does not explain why the park is better.

Activities are too vague – “play games” tells us nothing.

No linking words between ideas.

Ending is too short and feels rushed.

Time: 1.5 min. Click through each weakness. Response C is better than A but still needs work.

The Secret: Linking Words

Time: 1 min. Transition to the cohesion section. Focus on how “As for food” and “That way” connect ideas.

Without “As for food”

We could organise a treasure hunt.

Everyone could bring their favourite food.

The two topics jump with no connection.

Time: 1 min. Entry slide. Click once. Point out how the two topics feel disconnected.

With “As for food”

We could organise a treasure hunt.

As for food, everyone could bring their favourite food.

“As for food” bridges the two topics smoothly.

Time: 1 min. Auto-animate reveals the transition. “As for food” appears and connects the two topics.

Without “That way”

Everyone brings their favourite food.

We get more variety and less work for one person.

Cause and result feel disconnected.

Time: 1 min. Entry slide. The cause and result feel like separate thoughts.

With “That way”

Everyone brings their favourite food.

That way, we get more variety and less work for one person.

“That way” shows the reader the result.

Time: 1 min. Auto-animate reveals “That way” connecting the cause to the result.

Your Turn

Time: 1 min. Transition to the writing task.

Worksheet Task 3

Open your worksheet. Find the email from Tom.

You need to reply. Write about 100 words.

Tell Tom which place is better and why.

Suggest 2-3 sports to include.

Say yes or no to prizes and explain why.

Time: 1 min. Read through the prompt together. Identify the three notes.

Write Your Email

Write your reply to Tom on the worksheet.

About 100 words. Address all three notes.

Use “As for” or “That way” to connect your ideas.

Give reasons for your choices.

Time: 15 min. Students write independently. Teacher circulates.

That’s Your Email. Make It Flow.

Good luck with your exam!

Time: 30 sec. Wrap up. Remind students to use these techniques in the real PET exam.
