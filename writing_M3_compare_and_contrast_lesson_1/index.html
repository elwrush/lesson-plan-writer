Why is this lesson important?

  • Every essay question hides a topic and two opinions.
  • Clear writers read the question before they write.
  • Your opinion is the engine.
Frame: essay competitions, exams, and life all reward clear opinions.

How do we read the question?

Strategy: Read twice

DoRead the question twice.
WhyIt has four parts.
HowFind the topic. Spot both opinions. Choose one.
Model reading the conscription question aloud twice.

Every question has four parts

ContextThe topic. The situation.
Opinion AOne side of the argument.
Opinion BThe other side.
Your choiceWhich one do you agree with?
Point at each part. CCQ: Which part gives the topic?

Try it: Find the parts

Teacher example: Conscription

Match each sentence to its part.

Context
But others believe it wastes two years of young men's lives.
Opinion A
Personally, I think military service is an essential commitment.
Opinion B
Every year, Thai men must join the army for a short time.
Your choice
Some people say the army builds discipline and national pride.
Ask students to match each sentence to its part before you click.

Strategy: Take a position

DoChoose one side.
WhyEssays need one clear opinion.
HowAsk: which side do I agree with more?
Emphasize: no side is wrong, but you must choose.

Try it: Choose a side

Teacher example: Conscription
The teacher must choose a side.
 Military training builds discipline and national pride.
 Two years of a young man's life is a big waste.
Ask: which side would you choose? Then click.

How strong is your evidence?

Strategy: Use strong evidence

DoSupport your reasons with evidence.
WhyStrong evidence convinces the reader.
HowChoose from the four levels.
Stress: statistics are the strongest, anecdotes the weakest.

StatisticsA survey found that 7 in 10 young men feel fitter after army training.
QuotationsAn officer said, "Army life taught me to be strong."
News examplesThe news showed young men in uniform helping people after the floods.
Personal anecdotesMy uncle says the army made him fitter.

Write your response

Write your opinion. Add one reason. Add one piece of evidence.
Three sentences minimum. Collect the responses at the end of the lesson.
