## How to Teach Each Reading Skill (Pedagogical Approach)

### 1. Skimming — Stage 3 (Exercises B, C1, C2)

**B1-level definition:** Reading fast to get the general idea — not every word.

**Teaching mechanism (slide-strategy-skim):**
- Static pedagogical slide — all 4 steps visible. Teacher walks through each step verbally.
- Steps use the ACTUAL text as the worked example:
  - Step 1: "Look at the picture (man frying Kerak Telor) and heading (The Food of Indonesia). Guess: what is this text about?"
  - Step 2: "Read only the FIRST sentence of each paragraph. Don't read every word!" — show paragraph 1's first sentence on screen: *"Situated in a warm, tropical region, Indonesia has a lot of rain and sunshine and therefore has the perfect climate for a long growing season."*
  - Step 3: "Find repeated words." — keywords: farming, crops, food, Indonesia
  - Step 4: "Check the summary. Does it match what you found?" — students verify their C2 summary against their skim notes.

**Why this works for B1:** 
- Explicit instruction with a model (worked example principle)
- Sentence starters reduce cognitive load
- Concrete task: "find repeated words" is simpler than "identify themes"
- The text is right there — students don't need to recall from memory

### 2. True/False/Not Given — Stage 4 (Exercise D)

**B1-level definition:** 
- TRUE = the text says exactly this
- FALSE = the text says the OPPOSITE
- NOT GIVEN = the text doesn't say anything about this

**Teaching mechanism (slide-detail-step1 through 4):**
- 4-slide auto-animate strategy block using Sentence #1 from Exercise D as the worked example: *"Very few people now work in farming in Indonesia."* (Answer: FALSE)
- Step 1: Read the statement carefully. Note each claim. "Very few people" + "now work in farming"
- Step 2: Find key words → locate the paragraph. Keywords: "farming", "people", "work". These appear in paragraph 1.
- Step 3: Compare with text evidence. Auto-animate reveals a side-by-side comparison: 
  - Statement says: "Very few people work in farming"
  - Text says: "A large percentage of the population works in the farming industry" 
  - Auto-animate highlights the contradictory words in `#4fc3f7` blue
- Step 4: Decide. "Very few" ≠ "large percentage" → Answer: FALSE. Auto-animate reveals the final answer.

**NG trap prevention:** 
- Use Sentence #2 ("Indonesia does not produce any meat products") to model the NG category separately.
- Rule: "If you can't find the information in ANY paragraph → NOT GIVEN"
- Contrast with FALSE: "If you find the OPPOSITE → FALSE"

**Why auto-animate (not fragments) for the strategy:**
- The strategy is a step-by-step build-up. Each slide adds the next step while keeping previous steps visible but dimmed. This lets the teacher pause at each decision point.
- Fragments on a single slide would either hide previous steps (losing context) or show everything at once (overwhelming).
- The matching data-id attributes preserve visual continuity — students see the same statement text morphing as analysis gets added.

### 3. Critical Thinking — Stage 5 (Exercises E1, E2)

**B1-level definition:**
Connecting text information to personal experience and expressing opinions with simple reasons. NOT evaluating arguments or detecting bias — that's B2+.

**The E1 and E2 questions:**
- E1: "What changes in diet have there been in Indonesia? Have you had similar changes in diet in your country?"
- E2: "Why do you think people eat more processed foods today? What health problems might it cause?"

**Teaching mechanism (slide-strategy-think):**
- Static pedagogical slide — thinking frames as sentence starters:
  ```
  How to Discuss the Text:
  Step 1: Think — What did the text say? → "The text says that..."
  Step 2: Connect — Is it the same or different in my country? → "In my country, ..."
  Step 3: Explain — Why do I think that? → "I think... because..."
  Step 4: Discuss — Ask your partner: "What do you think?"
  ```
- **Modeled example using E1**: 
  - "The text says Indonesians now eat more dairy, meat, and sugar. In Thailand, we also eat more fast food than before. I think this happens because people are busier now and don't have time to cook."
  - This models text-to-self + cause-effect reasoning in B1-accessible language.

**Sentence starters on screen (slide-exE-task):**
- "I think... because..."
- "In my country, ..."  
- "This is similar because..." / "This is different because..."
- "One problem is..."

**Why static (not fragments) for critical thinking:**
- Discussion is open-ended — students need all frames visible as a reference
- Fragments would hide the thinking scaffolds, defeating their purpose
- The teacher's verbal facilitation structures the discussion pace, not slide clicks
