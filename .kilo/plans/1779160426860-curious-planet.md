### Shape D (PPP) Structure for This Topic

**Present (≈12 min):**
- Lead-in: Why do dictionaries use funny symbols? Show IPA chart from materials
- Target language: Selected IPA symbols (focus on consonants most relevant to Vietnamese learners: θ/ð, ʃ/ʒ, tʃ/dʒ, s/z distinctions)
- MFP: Sound→symbol→example word for each target phoneme

**Practice (≈22 min):**
- Controlled: Students match IPA transcriptions to English words
- Guided: Decode IPA sentences (use #2–5 as class examples, #6–8 as pair work)
- Audio check: Play Tape15.mp3 for self-correction after decoding

**Produce (≈12 min):**
- Freer: Students write IPA transcriptions for new sentences about themselves
- Peer dictation: Partner reads IPA, other writes the English
- Wrap-up: Review common errors, summary of symbols learned

## Special Requests — Full Lesson Flow

### Lead-in (~8 min): YouTube Video + Discussion

1. Show video: **"Pronouncing English Words in a Thai Accent 🥵 🇹🇭"** — `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ocvj99dC67E`
2. Pause at various mispronounced words. As a class:
   - Pronounce the word in Thai
   - Show how differently it's pronounced in English
3. Discussion: Why is English pronunciation so hard? Thai symbols often don't match English sounds → we need a special "alphabet" (the phonemic script) to show correct pronunciation
4. Write **"water"** on the board:
   - English spelling: `water`
   - IPA with stress: `/ˈwɔːtə(r)/`
   - Contrast: Thai pronunciation vs English → the phonemic script bridges the gap

### Presentation (~10 min): Phoneme Chart + Voiced/Voiceless

5. Work through the IPA chart from `symbols.md` together as a class:
   - Show which symbols have **Thai equivalents** (many consonants: p, b, t, d, k, m, n, ŋ, etc.)
   - Highlight symbols with **no Thai equivalent**: /θ/ /ð/ /ʃ/ /ʒ/ /tʃ/ /dʒ/ /v/ /z/ /ɜː/ /ə/
   - Introduce **voiced vs unvoiced** consonants — use the p/b, t/d, k/g pairs from the chart
   - Drill the difficult sounds (especially /θ/ think, /ð/ this, /ʃ/ shoe, /ʒ/ measure)

### Practice — Controlled (~10 min): Model + Guided Decoding

6. **Model** Sentence 1 (Tom likes writing poetry):
   - Show English + IPA side by side
   - Teacher reads the IPA aloud, students follow
   - Identify stress marks, schwa, and tricky symbols
7. **Guided practice** — Sentences 2–5 as a class:
   - Students attempt to read each IPA transcription aloud
   - Teacher corrects pronunciation
   - Reveal the English sentence after each attempt

### Practice — Freer (~8 min): Pair Work + Audio Check

8. **Pair work** — Sentences 6–8:
   - Students decode IPA in pairs, write the English
   - Pairs practice reading the IPA aloud to each other
9. **Audio check** — Play Tape15.mp3 ("T15 — Listen and check your answers")
   - Students self-correct their decoded sentences against the audio

### Produce (~8 min): Create + Peer Dictation

10. **Freer production**:
    - Students write IPA transcriptions for 2–3 new sentences about themselves or their classmates
    - Peer dictation: Partner A reads their IPA aloud, Partner B writes the English sentence
    - Swap roles

### Wrap-up (~2 min): Review

11. Quick quiz: Teacher shows IPA symbols, class says the sound
12. Summary: What we learned — IPA is a universal "alphabet" for sounds; some symbols match Thai, some are new; voiced vs unvoiced matters

### Total: 46 minutes

### Key Pedagogical Notes

- **Thai-learner focus**: Emphasize θ/ð, ʃ/ʒ, tʃ/dʒ, v, z — sounds with no Thai equivalent
- **Voiced/voiceless pairs**: Use the chart's natural pairing (p/b, t/d, k/g, f/v, s/z, θ/ð, ʃ/ʒ, tʃ/dʒ)
- **Stress marks**: Introduce ˈ (primary stress) with "water" example; reinforce in each sentence
- **Schwa /ə/**: The most common English vowel — highlight its frequency in unstressed syllables throughout the exercise
- **Video**: Serves as both humor/engagement AND the "need" for phonemic script (the cognitive hook)
