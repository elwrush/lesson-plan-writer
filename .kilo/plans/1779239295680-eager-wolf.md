### Practice 3 — Subjects, Verbs, and Objects

```typst
#line(length: 100%)

= Answer Key — Sentences and Capitalization, Part 2

== Practice 3 — Subjects, Verbs, and Objects (p. 14)

S = Subject (one underline), V = Verb (two underlines), O = Object (circle)

#table(
  columns: 4,
  table.header[*\#*][*Sentence*][*S / V / O*][*Notes*],
  [1], [My youngest brother is in high school.], [S: My youngest brother \ V: is \ O: (none)], [Linking verb — no object],
  [2], [He watches TV and does homework at the same time.], [S: He \ V: watches, does \ O: TV, homework], [Two verbs, two objects],
  [3], [He works at a shopping mall.], [S: He \ V: works \ O: (none)], [No object — "at a shopping mall" is a prepositional phrase],
  [4], [He likes his job but doesn't like his boss.], [S: He \ V: likes, doesn't like \ O: his job, his boss], [Two clauses — two objects],
  [5], [His job is easy and pays well.], [S: His job \ V: is, pays \ O: (none)], [Linking verb "is" — no object. "Pays" has no object here.],
  [6], [This semester, he is taking extra classes.], [S: he \ V: is taking \ O: extra classes], [Present continuous — one object],
  [7], [He will go to college next year.], [S: He \ V: will go \ O: (none)], [Phrasal verb "go to" — no object],
  [8], [He speaks and understands English very well.], [S: He \ V: speaks, understands \ O: English], [Two verbs share one object],
  [9], [On weekends, he and his friends play soccer.], [S: he and his friends \ V: play \ O: soccer], [Compound subject],
  [10], [He doesn't have a girlfriend yet.], [S: He \ V: doesn't have \ O: a girlfriend], [One object],
)
```

### Practice 4 — Editing for Subjects and Verbs (Larry's Bad Habit)

```typst
#line(length: 100%)

== Practice 4 — Editing for Subjects and Verbs (Larry's Bad Habit, p. 15)

Missing words shown in *bold*. Some answers may vary.

1. My friend Larry has a bad habit. (No missing word)
2. He #strong[is] never on time to anything. (Missing verb)
3. #strong[He] arrives ten minutes late everywhere. (Missing subject)
4. Larry #strong[has] always an excuse. (Missing verb)
5. "I missed the bus." (No missing word)
6. "My alarm clock didn't ring." (No missing word)
7. "My watch stopped." (No missing word)
8. "My mother telephoned me just as I was leaving." (No missing word)
9. He uses each excuse at least twice a week. (No missing word)
10. I know them all. (No missing word)
11. Whenever Larry rushes in — ten minutes late, of course — and starts to say, "Sorry I'm late, but I ... " or "Sorry I'm late, but my ... " or "Sorry I'm late, but my mother ... ," I can finish the sentence for him. (No missing word)
12. Larry's bad habit #strong[is] not a problem for me any longer. (Missing verb)
13. If the movie #strong[is] at 7:00, I tell Larry it starts at 6:45. (Missing verb)
14. Then he #strong[is] early! (Missing verb)

*Note:* Sentence 3 could also accept "Larry" as the subject (more natural). Sentence 12 accepts "is" but "has become" is also possible.
```
