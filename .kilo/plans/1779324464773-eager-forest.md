## Stage Structure (5 stages, 46 minutes total)

### Stage 1: Lead-in (8 min, Ss-Ss)
**Aim:** To activate learners' interest in George Lucas and cinema, and to raise awareness of typical Thai L1 subject-verb agreement errors

**Procedure:**
- Display a splash image of George Lucas. Elicit what students know about him (films, influence, technology). Build a word cloud of their contributions on the board.
- Present 6 sentences about George Lucas containing typical Thai L1 subject-verb agreement errors on the IWB.
- Students work in pairs to identify what's wrong with each sentence. Teacher monitors but does not correct.
- Auto-animate corrections to reveal the right forms. Elicit briefly why each correction was needed — but don't explain rules yet.

**The 6 lead-in sentences (Thai L1 errors → auto-animated corrections):**

| # | Error sentence | Corrected sentence | Error type |
|---|---------------|-------------------|------------|
| 1 | George Lucas make movies about space. | George Lucas **makes** movies about space. | Missing 3rd-person -s |
| 2 | The fans of Star Wars is very loyal. | The fans of Star Wars **are** very loyal. | Prepositional phrase trap |
| 3 | There is six Star Wars films in the original series. | There **are** six Star Wars films... | There + be agreement |
| 4 | Each of the films have a unique story. | Each of the films **has** a unique story. | Each = singular |
| 5 | Many Thai student loves Star Wars. | Many Thai **students love** Star Wars. | Many = plural; missing noun plural |
| 6 | A lot of the special effects is computer-made. | A lot of the special effects **are** computer-made. | A lot → countable plural |

### Stage 2: Test 1 — Diagnostic (8 min, S)
**Aim:** To diagnose learners' ability to identify and correct subject-verb agreement errors across the five key trouble spots before explicit teaching

**Procedure:**
- Distribute a bespoke 8-sentence error-correction handout (all sentences about George Lucas/film).
- Students work individually. Instruction: "Each sentence has ONE subject-verb agreement error. Find it and write the correction."
- Teacher circulates and notes which error types cause the most difficulty (prepositional phrase traps? there+be? each/one?). Hold all answers for the Teach stage.

**Bespoke diagnostic test — 8 items:**

| # | Sentence (with error) | Correction | Target rule |
|---|----------------------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | George Lucas have changed the film industry in many ways. | has | Third-person singular -s |
| 2 | There is two main characters in the original Star Wars. | are | There + be — subject follows |
| 3 | Each of the Star Wars movies have a different director. | has | Each = always singular |
| 4 | Many people in Thailand likes the Star Wars series. | like | Prepositional phrase trap + many = plural |
| 5 | The special effects in the first film was amazing for its time. | were | Prepositional phrase trap |
| 6 | A lot of young filmmakers admires George Lucas's work. | admire | A lot → countable plural |
| 7 | One of George Lucas's best films were American Graffiti. | was | One = always singular |
| 8 | The characters and the story of Star Wars appeals to all ages. | appeal | Compound subject = plural |

### Stage 3: Teach — Clarifying (12 min, T-Ss)
**Aim:** To draw out the five key rules of subject-verb agreement through guided analysis of model sentences, targeting only the problem areas observed in the diagnostic

**Procedure:**
- Return to the diagnostic test. Elicit which answers students were unsure about (show of hands for each item). Don't reveal correct answers — build suspense.
- Display the George Lucas model text from p10 on the IWB. Pull out these marker sentences:
  - "Filmmaker Lucas has changed the film industry in many ways." (simple SV)
  - "One new technology was a special computer-assisted camera crane." (one = singular)
  - "The Star Wars movies were international hits." (subject after article = plural)
  - "Young people and adults enjoyed them." (compound subject, plural)
- Elicit the generalisation for each rule before confirming. Board the five rules with example sentences:
  1. **Ignore prepositional phrases** — cross them out mentally: "The color *of her eyes* changes..."
  2. **There + be** — the subject follows be; look ahead: "There *are* several kinds..."
  3. **Always singular**: each, one, neither, either, anyone, nothing
  4. **Always plural**: both, several, many
  5. **Can be either**: some, all, most, a lot, none — check the noun in the prepositional phrase
- Use real examples from the textbook rules (pp12-13) to illustrate each rule. Students copy the five-rule summary into notebooks.

### Stage 4: Test 2 — Practice 3 (12 min, Ss-Ss)
**Aim:** To apply the five subject-verb agreement rules in controlled and freer practice, demonstrating improved accuracy after clarification

**Procedure:**
- **Part A (7 min):** Students complete Practice 3 Part A (items 1–11, p13) individually — choose the correct verb form. Pair check. Quick whole-class feedback using the IWB. Cross-reference answers against `answer_key.typ`.
- **Part B (5 min):** Students find and correct the six subject-verb agreement errors in the "Young Golf Stars" paragraph (Practice 3 Part B, textbook p14). Pairs compare corrections. Teacher reveals answers on the IWB.

### Stage 5: Wrap-up (6 min, T-Ss)
**Aim:** To consolidate the five agreement rules and confirm learners can produce accurate sentences independently

**Procedure:**
- Quick oral drill: teacher calls out a subject phrase, students respond chorally with the correct verb form (e.g., "Each of the students..." → "has"; "A lot of my friends..." → "like").
- Students write two original sentences about a favourite film or filmmaker, each using a different agreement rule from today's lesson. Two volunteers write theirs on the board for class checking.
- Recap the five rules from the board. Address any remaining questions.
