## Lesson 2: Scene Creation (46 min)

### Creative approach

**Techniques:** Persona-based writing, McKinsey-style problem solving

Students are given a problem: *"How can we tell this scene from another character's point of view?"* They form a hypothesis about what that character notices, values, and omits, then test it by writing the scene.

### Shape: F (Productive Skills) with McKinsey adaptation

| Stage | What happens | Time |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **1. Lead-in — Recall** | Quick recap of Chapter 2. Show the Outsiders clip again (19:08–23:30) to refresh characters. | 5 min |
| **2. Define the problem** | "Ponyboy tells this story. What if Cherry told it? What would SHE notice that Ponyboy missed?" Elicit how different characters see the same event differently. | 5 min |
| **3. Model analysis** | Show a short excerpt from Chapter 2 rewritten from Cherry's perspective (teacher-prepared). Students identify what changed: tone, vocabulary, what's included/omitted. | 5 min |
| **4. Group formation + hypothesise** | Groups of 4. Each group picks a lens (Cherry, Johnny, Dally, or Two-Bit). They form a hypothesis: *"When [character] tells this story, they will focus on ____ and change the tone to ____."* | 3 min |
| **5. Write** | Groups write their 4-minute scene. Teacher circulates, checks progress. The scene must be performable — script format with character names and dialogue. | 20 min |
| **6. Script check + rehearse** | Groups swap scripts with another group for a quick peer check. Groups rehearse silently. | 5 min |
| **7. Wrap-up — preview** | "Next lesson: table read then performance." Collect scripts for safekeeping. | 3 min |

### Materials
- The Outsiders Chapter 2 text (for reference)
- Outsiders drive-in clip (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvyYEWSK1-w&t=19m08s)
- Model rewritten excerpt (teacher-prepared, 1 paragraph from Cherry's perspective)
- Blank paper / Chromebooks for script writing

## Lesson 3: Table Read → Dress Rehearsal (46 min)

### Creative approach

**Techniques:** Drama/table-read format, Comparative input, low-stakes practice

Students do a table read (voices only) then a dress rehearsal (standing, with blocking) — but framed as practice for the Lesson 5 Showcase, not as a final performance. This lowers the stakes and lets them experiment. After the rehearsals, they collect peer feedback they'll use in Lesson 4 to choose their persona.

### Shape: F (Productive Skills)

| Stage | What happens | Time |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **1. Lead-in — Warm-up** | Voice warm-up: tongue twisters, character voice recall — "Say it as Dally. Now as Johnny." Quick reminder: "This is PRACTICE for the showcase. Experiment. Try things." | 5 min |
| **2. Rehearse** | Groups retrieve scripts. 8 min silent rehearsal. Teacher circulates. | 8 min |
| **3. Table read (Round 1)** | Each group does a seated table read in front of the class. Scripts in hand, voices only. Class watches for character voice. Each group gets 1 line of peer feedback after reading. | 15 min (3 min × 4 groups + transitions) |
| **4. Dress rehearsal (Round 2)** | Same scenes, now standing, with movement and blocking. Teacher records audio (or video) for Lesson 4 feedback analysis. | 8 min (2 min × 4 groups) |
| **5. Compare + feedback** | "Table read vs. Dress rehearsal: what changed?" Peer feedback on voice consistency across both formats. | 5 min |
| **6. Wrap-up — preview** | "Next lesson: we'll analyse these recordings and prepare for the SHOWCASE." Teacher collects the audio/video for Lesson 4. | 5 min |

### Materials
- Student scripts from Lesson 2
- Timer for performances
- Audio/video recording device (phone)

### Slides needed (~8)
1. Splash
2. Title — "Lights, Camera, Rehearse"
3. Warm-up — tongue twister + "This is practice — experiment"
4. Table read instructions — seated, voices only
5. Dress rehearsal — "Now with movement"
6. Compare — "Table read vs. Dress rehearsal: what changed?"
7. Peer feedback prompts — "One strength of the voice work was ____"
8. Wrap-up — preview Lesson 4 + showcase

## Lesson 4: Persona + 4/3/2 Retry (46 min)

### Creative approach

**Techniques:** Problem-first feedback (McKinsey-style), Persona-based speaking, Comparative input (4/3/2 self-comparison)

This lesson refines performance through persona-based practice. Students receive their dress rehearsal feedback, choose a persona that addresses their specific weakness, then perform repeatedly with the 4/3/2 shrinking-timer technique. The McKinsey arc is explicit:

1. **Define the problem** — "Here's your feedback. What's your one weakness?"
2. **Form a hypothesis** — "I think a [confident / calm / passionate] persona will fix it."
3. **Test** — 4/3/2 rounds with the persona
4. **Synthesise** — "What changed?"
5. **Recommend** — "Which persona will you use for the real test?"

### Shape: C (Test-Teach-Test)

| Stage | What happens | Time |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **1. Lead-in — Feedback return** | Return speaking feedback from Lesson 3 performances. Individuals read silently (2 min). Elicit patterns. | 5 min |
| **2. Define the problem (McKinsey Step 1–2)** | "Identify your ONE weakness — pace, volume, hesitation, intonation. Write it down." Students commit to a specific target. | 3 min |
| **3. Teach — Persona selection** | Show persona cards: The Confident Expert, The Calm Storyteller, The Passionate Advocate, The Charismatic Showman. Students match a persona to their weakness. "If your problem is hesitation, be The Confident Expert — they never hesitate." | 5 min |
| **4. Test 1 — 4 minutes** | Students give their speech/performance with their chosen persona. Partner times them. 4-minute target. | 5 min |
| **5. Test 2 — 3 minutes** | Same speech. Same persona. 3-minute target. Partner times. | 4 min |
| **6. Test 3 — 2 minutes** | Same speech. Same persona. 2-minute target. Partner times. | 3 min |
| **7. Synthesise (McKinsey Step 5–6)** | Pair discussion: "What changed across the three rounds? Did your persona help? What will you do differently next time?" | 5 min |
| **8. Share recommendations (McKinsey Step 7)** | Volunteers share: "My persona is ____. It helped me ____. Before the speaking test, I will ____." | 10 min |
| **9. Wrap-up — preview** | "Next lesson: the SHOWCASE. You've got your persona. You've tested it. Now show it to the audience." Students commit: "I will use the ____ persona in the showcase." | 6 min |

### Materials
- Speaking feedback slips from Lesson 3
- Persona cards (teacher-prepared: The Confident Expert, The Calm Storyteller, The Passionate Advocate, The Charismatic Showman)
- Timers (student phones or classroom clock)
- The speech/performance text (from Lesson 3 scripts or a new short prompt)

### Slides needed (~9)
1. Splash
2. Title — "Your Persona, Your Voice"
3. Feedback return — "Find your ONE weakness"
4. The McKinsey arc — Define → Hypothesise → Test → Synthesise → Recommend
5. Persona cards — 4 options with descriptions
6. 4/3/2 instructions — timer + persona reminder
7. Synthesise — "What changed across three rounds?"
8. Share — "I will use the ____ persona in the showcase."
9. End — preview Lesson 5 Showcase

## Lesson 5: The Outsiders — Live at C·E·L Mathayom (46 min)

### Creative approach

**Techniques:** Drama/performance, Meaningful culmination, Series bookend

The final lesson. Students aren't "presenting homework." They're premiering their scene. Each group introduces their creative choice, performs, and receives structured peer feedback. The loop closes: Lesson 1 asked "What if your voice could change who you are?" and introduced Heisenberg. Lesson 5 answers that question, and the students are the proof.

### Shape: F (Productive Skills) — production stage as showcase

| Stage | What happens | Time |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **1. Lead-in — Set the stage** | Dim the lights. Display the Heisenberg splash (same image from Lesson 1). "One week ago you watched Aaron Paul and wondered how he brought Jesse to life with just his voice. Today, you're Aaron Paul. Your classmates are your audience. Show them what you've created." | 3 min |
| **2. Group prep** | Final rehearsal. Scripts, blocking, voice warm-up. Persona reminder: "Remember — you're not you. You're the character." | 5 min |
| **3. Showcase — 4 groups** | Each group: 1 min introduction ("We wrote this from _'s perspective because..."), 3 min performance, 2 min peer feedback using structured prompts. | 24 min (6 min × 4) |
| **4. Award ceremony** | Class votes (hands up, eyes closed): Best Voice Work, Most Convincing Character, Best Creative Adaptation. Simple verbal recognition — teacher calls the winner and says why. | 5 min |
| **5. Reflection** | Return to the Lesson 1 question: "What if your voice could change who you are?" Students write one sentence. 2–3 volunteers share. | 5 min |
| **6. Series close** | Teacher closes the loop: "You started with flat voices and a fear of speaking. You're leaving with tools, characters, and the knowledge that you can choose who you are when you speak. That's not just ESL — that's power." | 4 min |

### Materials
- Student scripts (final versions from Lesson 4, polished with persona adjustments)
- Heisenberg splash image (same as Lesson 1, for series bookend)
- Peer feedback prompt cards
- Timer for performances

### Slides needed (~9)
1. Splash — Heisenberg, same as Lesson 1, for series bookend
2. Title — "The Outsiders: Live at C·E·L Mathayom"
3. Set the stage — callback to Lesson 1 table read moment
4. Performance order — group names + character lens
5. Peer feedback prompts — structured: "One strength was ____. One thing I'd try differently is ____."
6. Awards — Best Voice Work, Most Convincing Character, Best Creative Adaptation
7. Reflection — "What if your voice could change who you are? Did it?"
8. Series close — "You can choose who you are when you speak."
9. End
