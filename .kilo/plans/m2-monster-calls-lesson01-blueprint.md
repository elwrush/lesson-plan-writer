# Design Blueprint — M2 Lesson 1: A Monster Calls — Voice and Character Through Dramatic Reading

## Lesson Overview

**Class:** M2 (all M2 classes)
**Topic:** Voice and Character — Introduction to Dramatic Reading (via A Monster Calls)
**Duration:** 46 minutes
**CEFR:** A2/B1 (M2 level — simpler than M3's B2)
**Lesson shape:** E (Receptive Skills — Traditional)
**Source text:** A Monster Calls, Chapter 3 "School" (Patrick Ness)
**Epub source:** `inputs\ARCHIVE\READING_01_M3\A Monstall Calls2.epub`

## Creative Approach: Breaking Bad "Show-Don't-Tell" Method

Same structure as M3 Lesson 1:
1. **Splash** — Heisenberg image primes "character transformation" metaphor
2. **Lead-in** — Speaking feedback patterns surfaced as fragments
3. **Reframe** — "Speaking = Acting" with boxed keyword "voice is your instrument"
4. **Pre-watch** — Define table read, set focused viewing task
5. **Primary input** — Breaking Bad table read (MqfSr8Gu9zI)
6. **Comparative input** — Same scene, filmed version (3A-Ck_2Na6s)
7. **Guided noticing** — Comparison table with fragment reveals
8. **Principle** — "getting into a role" — the character frees you from self-consciousness
9. **Personalize** — "What if you weren't YOU?"
10. **Red transition** — Phase change: Breaking Bad → A Monster Calls
11. **Secondary input** — A Monster Calls movie clip: "Conor Gets Attacked" (GstG6pDDnX8) — the EXACT Chapter 3 scene
12. **Characters** — Fragment reveals: Conor, Harry, Lily, Sully, Anton, Miss Kwan
13. **Task** — Chapter 3 group read-aloud with character voice assignments
14. **Voice tips** — Boxed keywords: PACE, VOLUME, EMOTION, PAUSES (simplified for M2)
15. **Wrap-up** — One-word reflection + preview

## Chapter 3 Characters & Voice Descriptors (from epub text)

**Full text verified.** Chapter 3 "School" — ~900 words, 6 speaking characters + teacher narration (Conor's internal thoughts).

### Scene summary
Conor arrives at school. Harry trips him. Conor bites his lip, tastes blood. Harry says calmly: "Be careful of the steps there. You might fall." Lily charges in: "Leave him alone!" Anton calls her a poodle. Sully shouts: "He'll have to get his baldy mother to kiss it better for him!" Lily pushes Sully into the bushes. Miss Kwan storms over. Lily says they were making fun of Conor's mother. Miss Kwan asks Conor: "Is this true?" Conor — swallowing blood, burning inside — lies: "No, Miss, it's not true. I just fell. They were helping me up." Lily is devastated. Harry smiles: "Well done, O'Malley."

### Character roles for read-aloud

| Character | Voice descriptor | Key lines | Read by |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|---------|
| **Narrator** | Thoughtful, dark, observant — Conor's internal voice: the nightmare, the burning sun in his stomach, the secret mark Harry can see | "He could already taste the blood in his mouth..." / "Somewhere over the past year, something had changed..." / "Conor's stomach contracted to a ball of fire, like a little sun burning him up from the inside..." | **Teacher** |
| **Conor** | One line. The entire chapter hangs on it. Swallowing blood, lying to protect his bully, betraying Lily. Deliver it flat, controlled, final. | "No, Miss, it's not true. I just fell. They were helping me up." | Student 1 |
| **Harry** | Calm, amused, menacing. The voice of someone who knows he's won. Never raises his voice — that's what makes him scary. | "Be careful of the steps there. You might fall." / "You're bleeding, O'Malley." / "Well done, O'Malley." | Student 2 |
| **Lily** | Furious, brave, then hurt into silence. Starts loud and righteous, ends with no words at all — just a dropped-open mouth. | "Leave him alone!" / "I'm just making it a fair fight." / "They were making fun of Conor's mother!" / (then silence — the most powerful moment) | Student 3 |
| **Sully** | Sneering and mean, then plays the victim. Shift from bully to "I might need to go home" — the voice of a coward. | "He'll have to get his baldy mother to kiss it better for him!" / "I don't know, Miss. I might need to go home." | Student 4 |
| **Anton** | Laughing sidekick. One line, all mockery. | "Your poodle's here to save you." | Student 5 |
| **Miss Kwan** | Stern, serious, authority. Not angry — grave. Her voice carries the weight of the school. | "Lillian Andrews!" / "Don't milk it." / "Is this true, Conor?" | Student 6 |

### The Masterclass Moment
Conor's single line is the dramatic climax. The student playing Conor has ONE job: deliver it with the weight of someone who knows exactly what he's doing — throwing away his only ally, burning inside, swallowing blood. This is where "getting into a role" becomes real. The teacher can stop here: "That line. That ONE line. He's not being (student's name). He's being Conor. The character frees him to say something terrible — and mean it."
