## Pairing & Role Assignment (directive — embedded across stages)

### Teacher-assigned pairs
- Teacher assigns pairs, NOT free choice. These are the same pairs from the previous lesson (each pair already planned a podcast together last session).
- Ss sit with their assigned partner immediately upon entering the room.

### Three role combinations — students choose ONE
On the board or slide, present three role combinations clearly. Ss choose one as a pair before script writing.

| Role | Combination A (Co-hosts) | Combination B (Host + Guest) | Combination C (Debate Duo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Student 1** | Opens the podcast, delivers Argument 1 (30–40 sec) | **Host**: Opens the show, introduces the guest, asks 2 prepared questions (30–40 sec) | Delivers Argument FOR the topic (30–40 sec) |
| **Student 2** | Delivers Argument 2, closes the podcast (30–40 sec) | **Guest**: Answers the host's questions with personal examples and advice (30–40 sec) | Delivers Argument AGAINST or a counterpoint, closes the podcast (30–40 sec) |
| **Total** | Two equal contributions | Interview format — the guest has the main content, host facilitates | Two opposing or contrasting takes |

**Choosing roles**: After the teacher presents the three combinations, each pair has 30 seconds to decide. If a pair can't decide, the teacher assigns Combination A by default.

**Both students MUST speak approximately 30–45 seconds each** regardless of combination.

## Lesson Structure & Timing (46 min)

### Stage 1 — Lead-in (4 min)
- **As Ss enter**: "Sit next to your podcast partner from last lesson. Pull out your planning sheet."
- Quick recall on the board: What did we learn about YouTubers and how they influence people? Take 2–3 rapid-fire answers.
- Announce today's goal: "You're going to record your podcast today — 60 to 90 seconds. This is your baseline. No retakes, no corrections. Just you, speaking."
- Emphasise: "This is your starting point for the year. We'll listen back in a few months and you'll see how far you've come."

### Stage 2 — Role Assignment & Useful Language (6 min)
- **Display the three role combinations (A/B/C)** on the board or slide as a table.
- Walk through each combination in 30 seconds each — name, what Student 1 does, what Student 2 does.
- **Directive**: "You have 30 seconds to choose. Decide with your partner now. If you can't decide, you'll do Combination A. Go."
- Count down aloud. After 30 seconds, ask: "All decided? Thumbs up if yes." Confirm all pairs have a combination.
- **Transition to language**: Display the functional phrases on the board in three categories aligned with the roles:
  - **Openings** (all combinations): "Welcome to my podcast…", "Today I'm going to talk about…", "I'm joined today by…"
  - **Persuading / giving advice** (Combinations A, B): "Have you ever thought about…?", "You might not realise this, but…", "Here's something to consider…"
  - **Structuring / contrasting** (all combinations): "First of all…", "Another point is…", "On the other hand…", "So, to sum up…"
- Quick choral drilling for rhythm and natural intonation (3 rounds, 60 seconds each: all-class, then rows, then volunteers).
- Ss annotate their planning sheet with the phrases relevant to their chosen combination.

### Stage 3 — Controlled Practice (7 min)
- **Exercise A — Mini-monologue drill**: Remaining in their assigned pairs.
  - **Round 1 (3 min)**: Student 1 delivers a 30-second mini-monologue on any topic (e.g., "Why you should watch Squid Game") using at least 2 of the target phrases. Student 2 listens and counts the phrases used.
  - **Round 2 (3 min)**: Switch. Student 2 now delivers a 30-second mini-monologue on a different topic. Student 1 counts the phrases.
  - **Quick feedback (1 min)**: Teacher calls out 2 strong examples heard during monitoring. Correct one common error (e.g., flat intonation — model a podcast delivery vs. reading aloud).

### Stage 4 — Script Preparation (12 min)
- **Strict 12-minute countdown on the board.**
- **Directive**: "Use your planning sheet from last lesson. Write a script that matches your chosen combination."
  - Combination A: Student 1 writes the opening + Argument 1. Student 2 writes Argument 2 + the closing.
  - Combination B: Student 1 (Host) writes the opening, introduction of guest, and 2 questions. Student 2 (Guest) writes the answers — personal examples and advice.
  - Combination C: Student 1 writes Argument FOR. Student 2 writes Argument AGAINST + the closing.
- Script requirements on the board:
  - ✅ Clear opening (who you are, what the topic is)
  - ✅ At least 2 arguments/advice points
  - ✅ At least 3 target phrases from Stage 2
  - ✅ A short closing ("Thanks for listening", "Until next time")
  - ✅ Total length: 60–90 seconds when spoken
- Teacher circulates aggressively — pushes for natural language, checks scripts are within the time window, reminds Ss of the countdown at 8 min, 4 min, and 2 min remaining.

### Stage 5 — Rehearsal (5 min)
- **Directive**: "You have 5 minutes to rehearse. Time yourself."
- **Round 1 (2 min)**: Practice with scripts in hand. Partner gives feedback: "Too fast? Too slow? Can you hear the target phrases?"
- **Round 2 (2 min)**: Practice again, this time aiming for limited script use — look up, sound conversational.
- **Final 60 seconds**: "One last run. Partner, hold up your hand when 60 seconds have passed so they know if they're on track."
- Teacher observes 2–3 pairs, gives rapid-fire coaching: "Louder opening." "You're at 50 seconds — you need more." "That phrase sounded natural — keep it."

### Stage 6 — Podcast Recording — Baseline (10 min)
- **Recording setup**: Teacher places recording device (phone/laptop) at the front. "One take only. No restarts. This is your baseline."
- **Order**: "Listen for your pair name. When called, come to the front, deliver your podcast, sit down. Everyone else: absolutely silent."
- Each pair comes up, delivers their podcast (60–90 seconds).
- Teacher records but gives no verbal feedback, corrections, or gestures during recording.
- Between recordings, teacher checks the timer and calls the next pair immediately — no dead air.
- If time runs short, remaining pairs record at the start of the next lesson. Prioritise pairs who are ready.

### Stage 7 — Wrap-up (2 min)
- Quick reflection displayed on the board: "What was the hardest part? What went better than you expected?"
- Ss discuss with their partner for 60 seconds. No whole-class feedback — this is a private reflection.
- Final statement from teacher: "That recording is your starting point. In a few months, you'll listen back and see how far you've come. Well done today. Good pace, good focus."
- Dismiss.
