Do you push the last sound of words too hard?
Listen to a student. What do you notice?

Lesson Objectives

1. Identify when Thai speakers push the last syllable too hard
2. Understand why this happens (the Thai stress rule)
3. Practise marking and saying words with correct stress

Introduce the three objectives. Students will notice a problem, understand why it happens, and practise fixing it.

Let’s Listen

Phase change. Moving from introduction to the listening task.

Exercise A: Listen to a Student

A student was asked: “What makes you stressed?”

Task: Listen the first time. Just listen.

Play the clip once with no task. Second play: students underline overstressed words on worksheet Exercise A. Pair check. Board the words.

What Did You Find?

Which words sound “too strong” at the end?

The student said… Problem word
“my teach-er teach me” teacher – she says “teach-ER”
“the ans-wer” answer – she says “ans-WER”
“an-oth-er story” another – she says “an-oth-ER”
“a bor-ing lesson” boring – she says “bor-ING”
“sto-ry” story – she says “sto-RY”

Use the click-table to reveal each word one by one. Elicit from students before clicking.

Why Does This Happen?

Shift from “what” to “why”. The Thai rule explains the root cause.

In Thai

O—O—O—STRONG

Every word: last part always strong

คอมพิวเตอร์ (computer) – weak weak STRONG

Thai fixed stress: the last syllable is always strong. Students can feel this with the Thai example.

In English

O—o—O—o

Every word: stress can be anywhere

TEACH-er | ANS-wer

English is different. The strong part can move. You have to learn each word.

Let’s Practise

Moving from theory to practice.

Stress Markers

We use the O o (big circle / small circle) method:

  • O = strong syllable (big circle)
  • o = weak syllable (small circle)

teacher -> O o boring -> O o understand -> o o O

Demo on board first. Then point to Exercise C on worksheet.

Exercise C: Mark the Stress

On your worksheet Exercise C, mark the stress for each sentence.

  1. My teacher gave us a boring lesson.
  2. I don’t understand the answer.
  3. Another student told me a story.
  4. My favourite subject is history.
  5. Please explain the problem carefully.

Circle every word with more than one syllable. Mark O for strong, o for weak.

7 minutes. Students work individually. Teacher circulates. Board answers for self-correction.

Check Your Answers

Self-correction. Then choral drill.

Exercise D: Listen to a London Speaker

A 16-year-old student from London reads the same sentences.

As you listen: Point to the stress patterns on your worksheet.

After listening: Exercise D – write one difference between the two speakers.

Play the London model audio. Each sentence is played twice with gaps. Students point to stress patterns as they hear them.

Exercise E: Write Your Own Sentences

On your worksheet Exercise E, write 3 sentences.

  1. Write about a school subject you like or don’t like.
  2. Write about something a teacher said to you yesterday.
  3. Write about a hobby you have.

Then: Circle every word with more than one syllable. Mark the stress using O o.

7 minutes. Students write individually. After writing, they mark their own stress patterns.

Read to Your Partner

Read your sentences to a partner.

Listen: Did your partner put the stress in the right place?

3 minutes. Student A reads, Student B checks. Swap.

One Takeaway

Tell your partner one thing you learned today.

Next time you speak English:

Don’t push the last sound. Let your voice end softly.

Quick pair share. Then collect worksheets.
