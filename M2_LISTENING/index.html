Should "smart glasses" be allowed?
Let's listen and make a decision.
Greet students. Ask: What are smart glasses? Do you know anyone with them?

Why is this lesson important?

  • You will practise listening to a song and a news report.
  • You will hear the debate about smart glasses and privacy.
  • You will make a decision: should they be allowed?

Opinion — Reason — Evidence

Students speculate about the song from the picture (a woman in a diner). Use Opinion-Reason-Evidence: I think... because...

A famous song

  • Suzanne Vega wrote the song in 1982.
  • Engineers used it to test the MP3 music format.
  • In 1990, a remix became an international hit.
  • It sold millions of copies around the world.
Cultural impact: the MP3 test connection. Economic success: the 1990 DNA remix sold millions. Keep it brief.

First listen — What is the song about?

What is Suzanne Vega trying to communicate?

AThe excitement of a busy city.
BThe quiet story of an ordinary moment.
CThe sadness of a rainy morning.

Answer

AnswerB — The quiet story of an ordinary moment.
WhyA woman sits in a diner. She watches people and reads the paper. It is an ordinary morning told as a story.
Confirm B. The song narrates a normal morning — a woman in a diner watching the world go by.

Main Lesson — Meta Smart Glasses

Transition to the main lesson. Say: Now we listen to a news report about smart glasses. The worksheet has the questions.

Listen 1 — The big idea

Watch the video once. Don't write anything.

The video is mainly about…

AWhere to buy smart glasses.
BWhat smart glasses can do, and why some people worry.
CHow smart glasses are made.
DWhy smart glasses are expensive.

Gist — Answer

AnswerB — What smart glasses can do, and why some people worry.
WhyThe video shows the features, then the privacy debate: people call them stalker glasses and want them banned.
Confirm B. The report covers both the benefits and the privacy concerns.

Listen 2 — Details

Watch again. Answer the questions on your worksheet.

True or False — Correct the False Ones

Write T or F on your worksheet.

If the sentence is false, correct it on the line.

Students complete the true/false section from the second listen. Timer pill: 4 minutes (240 seconds).

True or False — Answers

Discuss — Should Smart Glasses Be Banned?

Give your opinion. Agree or disagree. Reach a conclusion.

OpinionI think smart glasses should be banned because…
AgreeI agree with you because… That's a good point.
DisagreeI disagree because… I see what you mean, but…
ConclusionSo we agree that… In conclusion, we think…

Opinion — Reason — Evidence

Timer pill: 5 minutes (300 seconds). Use the worksheet outline (opinion + three ideas) and the language table. Groups report their conclusion.

Questions?

Wrap up. Elicit each group's conclusion. Ask: Which listening words were new for you?
