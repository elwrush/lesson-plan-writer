#  {#slide-vocab-threshold}

/ˈθrɛʃhoʊld/

[**threshold**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[A small rise in temperature is not El Niño — the water must reach a certain point. Past that [**threshold**]{.box}, El Niño has officially begun.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
Concept check: "If temperature is above the threshold, has El Niño officially started? (Yes)"
:::

#  {#slide-vocab-teaming-up}

/ˈtiːmɪŋ ʌp/

[**teaming up**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[Rain alone is not enough. Wind alone is not enough. When they [**team up**]{.box}, they create something much bigger.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
"When things team up, do they work together or against each other? (Together)"
:::

#  {#slide-vocab-oscillation}

/ˌɒsɪˈleɪʃən/

[**oscillation**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[Unlike a clock that ticks in one direction, the El Niño / La Niña cycle [**oscillates**]{.box} back and forth — warm to cool, then back to warm again.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
"Oscillation means a back-and-forth cycle. What natural cycle does ENSO refer to? (El Niño / La Niña)"
:::

#  {#slide-vocab-blob}

/blɒb/

[**blob**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[You could swim through this [**blob**]{.box} and not notice where it starts or ends — a giant patch of warm water stretching across the ocean.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
"Is a blob a large mass or a tiny drop? (A large mass)"
:::

#  {#slide-vocab-trigger}

/ˈtrɪɡər/

[**trigger**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[Like pulling a switch that starts a machine, El Niño does not cause the flood itself — it [**triggers**]{.box} the conditions for extreme weather.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
"If something is a trigger, does it start something or stop something? (It starts it)"
:::

#  {#slide-vocab-moisture}

/ˈmɔɪstʃər/

[**moisture**]{.fragment .answer-reveal}

[After a hot shower, you can see it on the mirror. In the air, you cannot see it — but this invisible [**moisture**]{.box} makes the air feel wet and heavy.]{.fragment .white-reveal}

::: notes
"Is moisture dry or wet? (Wet — water in the air)"
:::
