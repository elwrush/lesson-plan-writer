Let’s learn some words

Time: 5 min. Interaction: T-Ss. Six words from the transcript. Phonemic script first, click to reveal word, click for context. Drill pronunciation.

/ˈθrɛʃhoʊld/

threshold

A small rise in temperature is not El Niño — the water must reach a certain point. Past that threshold, El Niño has officially begun.

Concept check: “If temperature is above the threshold, has El Niño officially started? (Yes)”

/ˈtiːmɪŋ ʌp/

teaming up

Rain alone is not enough. Wind alone is not enough. When they team up, they create something much bigger.

“When things team up, do they work together or against each other? (Together)”

/ˌɒsɪˈleɪʃən/

oscillation

Unlike a clock that ticks in one direction, the El Niño / La Niña cycle oscillates back and forth — warm to cool, then back to warm again.

“Oscillation means a back-and-forth cycle. What natural cycle does ENSO refer to? (El Niño / La Niña)”

/blɒb/

blob

You could swim through this blob and not notice where it starts or ends — a giant patch of warm water stretching across the ocean.

“Is a blob a large mass or a tiny drop? (A large mass)”

/ˈtrɪɡər/

trigger

Like pulling a switch that starts a machine, El Niño does not cause the flood itself — it triggers the conditions for extreme weather.

“If something is a trigger, does it start something or stop something? (It starts it)”

/ˈmɔɪstʃər/

moisture

After a hot shower, you can see it on the mirror. In the air, you cannot see it — but this invisible moisture makes the air feel wet and heavy.

“Is moisture dry or wet? (Wet — water in the air)”
