# Cambridge B1 Preliminary for Schools — Writing Guide

> Compiled from the official Cambridge English *B1 Preliminary for Schools Teacher's Handbook* (168143).
> This is the authoritative reference — all assessment descriptors, task formats, and sample answers are taken directly from the handbook.
> Use when teaching PET-style article writing (Writing Part 2).

---

## 1. Exam Overview

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Paper** | Writing (Paper 2) |
| **Time** | 45 minutes total (both parts) |
| **Part 1** | Email (100–120 words) — 20 marks |
| **Part 2** | Article **or** Story (around 100 words) — 20 marks |
| **Article task** | Opinion-based, written for a magazine or website — candidates share personal experiences and express opinions |

In the article task, candidates are given a topic and must:
- Express their opinion clearly
- Address the reader directly
- Share personal information or experiences
- Organise ideas into paragraphs

---

## 2. Assessment Criteria (4 subscales, each worth 25%)

### Band 5 Descriptors — the target for strong B1 performance

| Criterion | Band 5 performance |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Content** | All content is relevant to the task. Target reader is **fully informed**. |
| **Communicative Achievement** | Uses the conventions of the task to **hold the target reader's attention** and communicate **straightforward ideas**. Register is appropriate. |
| **Organisation** | Text is **generally well-organised and coherent**, using a **variety of linking words and cohesive devices**. Paragraphs are used appropriately. |
| **Language** | Uses a **range of everyday vocabulary** appropriately, with occasional inappropriate use of less common lexis. Uses a **range of simple and some complex grammatical forms** with a good degree of control. Errors do not impede communication. |

### Band 3 Descriptors (minimum pass-level for B1)

| Criterion | Band 3 performance |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Content** | Minor irrelevances and/or omissions may be present. Target reader is **on the whole informed**. |
| **Communicative Achievement** | Uses the conventions of the communicative task in **generally appropriate ways** to communicate straightforward ideas. |
| **Organisation** | Text is **connected and coherent**, using **basic linking words** and a **limited number** of cohesive devices. |
| **Language** | Uses **everyday vocabulary** generally appropriately, while occasionally **overusing** certain lexis. Uses **simple grammatical forms** with a good degree of control. While errors are noticeable, meaning can still be determined. |

## 3. Article Conventions (from the handbook)

The official handbook states that article writing conventions include:
- **Sharing personal information** and experiences
- **Expressing opinions** clearly
- **Addressing the reader directly** to hold their attention
- Writing in a register suitable for the target reader (usually a magazine/website audience — informal-friendly)

The handbook's Band 5 sample article (Candidate C) uses a flexible structure — not a rigid formula. Adapt the paragraphing to the content:

```
PARAGRAPH 1 — INTRODUCTION
- Hook the reader with a personal statement or question
- Introduce the topic
- State your overall opinion

BODY PARAGRAPHS — IDEAS + EXAMPLES
- One main idea per paragraph
- Support with personal examples, reasons, or descriptions
- Use a mix of linking words and cohesive devices

FINAL PARAGRAPH — CONCLUSION / WIDER POINT
- Summarise your opinion or add a final thought
- Optionally broaden to a general point about the topic
```

```
PARAGRAPH 1 — INTRODUCTION
- Address the reader directly (question or statement)
- Introduce the topic
- State your overall opinion

PARAGRAPH 2 — FIRST REASON / EXAMPLE
- Topic sentence stating your first point
- Supporting example or reason
- Personal experience if relevant

PARAGRAPH 3 — SECOND REASON / EXAMPLE
- Topic sentence stating your second point
- Supporting example or reason
- May contrast with or build on Paragraph 2

PARAGRAPH 4 — CONCLUSION
- Summarise your opinion
- Final thought or recommendation to the reader
```

**Aim for ~100 words total.** Each body paragraph should be roughly equal in length.

## 4. Linking Words and Cohesive Devices

The handbook **distinguishes** between two levels:

| Category | Definition | Examples |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **Linking words** | Higher-frequency items providing explicit linkage | *and, but, because, first of all, finally* |
| **Cohesive devices** | More sophisticated linking + grammatical devices (reference pronouns, substitution, ellipsis, repetition) | *moreover, as a result, however, on the other hand, therefore* plus *it, this, one, there* for referencing |

Band 5 requires a **variety** of both. Band 3 uses **basic linking words** and a **limited number** of cohesive devices.

### Expressing opinions

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| In my opinion, … | Linking |
| I believe (that) … | Linking |
| I think (that) … | Linking |
| It seems to me that … | Cohesive |
| From my point of view, … | Cohesive |
| As far as I'm concerned, … | Cohesive |
| My view is that … | Cohesive |
| I strongly believe (that) … | Cohesive |
| The way I see it, … | Cohesive |

### Adding information

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| and … | Linking |
| also … | Linking |
| In addition, … | Cohesive |
| Moreover, … | Cohesive |
| Furthermore, … | Cohesive |
| as well as … | Cohesive |

### Contrasting

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| but … | Linking |
| However, … | Cohesive |
| Although … | Cohesive |
| On the other hand, … | Cohesive |
| Despite this, … | Cohesive |
| whereas … | Cohesive |

### Giving reasons and results

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| because … | Linking |
| so … | Linking |
| Therefore, … | Cohesive |
| As a result, … | Cohesive |
| This means that … | Cohesive |

### Sequencing

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| First of all, … | Cohesive |
| Secondly, … | Cohesive |
| Finally, … | Cohesive |

### Giving examples

| Phrase | Type |
|--------|------|
| for example, … | Cohesive |
| such as … | Cohesive |
| For instance, … | Cohesive |

### Referencing (grammatical cohesive devices)

The handbook highlights referencing pronouns as a key cohesive device:
- *I love to watch comedies **because it** makes me laugh.*
- ***The comedy** I love the most is Chinese Running Man. **In the show**, famous actors …*
- *The storylines are very interesting **and they** always tickles my funny bone.*

## 5. Complex Grammatical Forms (from the handbook)

The handbook defines **complex grammatical forms** as: noun clauses, relative and adverb clauses, subordination, passive forms, infinitives, verb patterns, modal forms, and tense contrasts.

These should be combined with **simple grammatical forms** (words, phrases, basic tenses, simple clauses).

### Examples from the Band 5 sample article

| Structure | Example from handbook sample |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Modal verbs** | *actors **must** overcome some challenging quests* / *Laughing **can** help us to release stress* / *It **may** also make us more attractive* |
| **Relative clause** | *The comedy **I love the most** is the Chinese Running Man* |
| **Infinitives** | *help us **to release** stress* |
| **-ing forms** | ***Laughing** out loud is great* / ***Laughing** can help us* |
| **Contrast within sentence** | *The storylines are very interesting **and** they always tickles my funny bone* |
| **Such as + gerund** | *such as **trading** a coffee bean … and **racing** in the mud* |

### Simple vs. Complex at B1

| Type | Handbook definition | Example |
|------|--------------------|---------|
| Simple forms | Words, phrases, basic tenses, simple clauses | *I love to watch comedies* |
| Complex forms | Noun/relative/adverb clauses, subordination, passive, infinitives, verb patterns, modals, tense contrasts | *actors **must overcome** challenging quests, **such as trading** a coffee bean* |

A mix of **simple and complex** sentences is expected:

### Simple sentences (accurate = safe)
- *Many teenagers spend hours on social media.*
- *I believe this is not a fair label.*

### Compound sentences (with *and*, *but*, *so*, *or*)
- *Teens care about their friends, **and** they also worry about global problems.*
- *Some people say teens are selfish, **but** I disagree.*

### Complex sentences (show range)

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Relative clause** | *Teenagers **who have close friends** feel more supported.* |
| **-ing form as subject** | *Having a good friend makes a big difference.* |
| **Modal verbs** | *Close friendships **can** help teens feel more confident.* |
| **Conditional (1st)** | *If you have a true friend, you feel less alone.* |
| **Although / While clause** | *Although some teens seem self-focused, many care deeply about others.* |

## 6. Common Errors to Avoid

| Error type | Wrong | Right |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Sentence fragment | *Because friends help each other.* | *Friends are important **because** they help each other.* |
| Run-on sentence | *Friends are important they help us.* | *Friends are important **because** they help us.* |
| Comma splice | *I have a best friend, we talk every day.* | *I have a best friend, **and** we talk every day.* |
| Missing subject | *Is very important to have friends.* | ***It** is very important to have friends.* |
| Wrong register | Mixing formal/informal | Keep a consistent informal-friendly tone for articles |

## 7. Official Handbook Sample Article — Band 5 (Candidate C)

**Prompt:** *What makes you laugh? Write an article telling us what you find funny and who you enjoy laughing with. Do you think it's good to laugh a lot? Why?*

> I love to watch comedies a lot because it makes me laugh. The comedy I love the most is the Chinese Running Man. I enjoy watching and laughing it with my family. In the show, famous actors and actresses must overcome some challenging quests, such as trading a coffee bean with someone else for something more expensive and racing in the mud to capture the flag. The storylines are very interesting and they always tickles my funny bone. Laughing out loud is great! Laughing can help us to release stress and make us feel better. It may also make us more attractive too!

| Subscale | Mark | Examiner commentary |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| Content | 5 | All content relevant. Fully informed about what they find funny, who they laugh with. Final point developed well. |
| Comm. Achievement | 5 | Conventions of article used (sharing personal info, expressing opinions). Holds reader's attention. Clear introduction. Balance between personal feeling and wider effects. |
| Organisation | 5 | Generally well organised and coherent. Variety of linking words and cohesive devices — especially referencing to avoid repetition (*because it, The comedy I love the most, it, In the show, such as, and they*). |
| Language | 5 | Range of everyday vocab + less common lexis (*comedies, challenging quests, trading, capture the flag, tickles my funny bone, release stress*). Range of simple + complex forms (modals: *must overcome, can help, may make*). Errors do not impede. |

## 8. 5-Point Band 5 Checklist

Before submitting, students should check:

1. **Content** — Did I answer the question? Did I include everything asked for? Is my target reader fully informed?
2. **Communicative Achievement** — Does my writing use article conventions (personal voice, opinions, reader address)? Is the register consistent?
3. **Organisation** — Did I use paragraphs? Did I use a mix of basic linking words AND more sophisticated cohesive devices? Is there a logical flow?
4. **Language** — Did I use a range of everyday vocabulary? Did I attempt some less common words/phrases? Did I mix simple and complex grammar (modals, relative clauses, -ing forms)?
5. **Accuracy** — Did I check spelling, verb endings, and word order? Are my errors minor/non-impeding?

## 9. Handbook Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Official definition |
|------|--------------------|
| **Target reader** | The hypothetical reader set up in the task (e.g., a magazine's readership, your English teacher) |
| **Conventions of the task** | Genre, format, register, and function appropriate to the task type |
| **Register** | The tone of writing — must be appropriate for the task and target reader |
| **Linking words** | Higher-frequency items providing explicit linkage (*and, but, because, first of all, finally*) |
| **Cohesive devices** | More sophisticated linking (*moreover, as a result*) + grammatical devices (reference pronouns, substitution, ellipsis, repetition) |
| **Simple grammatical forms** | Words, phrases, basic tenses, simple clauses |
| **Complex grammatical forms** | Noun clauses, relative/adverb clauses, subordination, passive forms, infinitives, verb patterns, modal forms, tense contrasts |
| **Non-impeding errors** | Errors that do not affect communication — not necessarily penalised |
| **Overuse** | Repeatedly using the same word because the candidate lacks resources to vary it |

## 10. Teacher Tips (from the official handbook)

- **Model the task**: Walk through one full example (from prompt to finished essay) before asking students to write. Use the official Band 5 sample article (Section 7) as a reference.
- **Use the checklist**: Give students the Band 5 checklist (Section 8) for peer and self-assessment.
- **Practise evaluating**: Have students look at sample answers and identify what the writer did well and what they could improve in terms of organisation, language, and communication — this is directly from the handbook's "How to approach" advice.
- **Sentence combining**: Practice turning simple sentences into compound/complex ones as a warm-up activity. Focus on the complex forms listed in the handbook (modals, relative clauses, -ing forms).
- **Transitions focus**: Distinguish between **linking words** (basic: *and, but, because*) and **cohesive devices** (more sophisticated: *however, therefore, moreover*). Students need both for Band 5.
- **Timed practice**: In later lessons, set 20–25 minutes for the article to simulate exam conditions.
- **Encourage ambition**: The handbook says candidates should "show the full range of their language ability and be ambitious in their use of language." Non-impeding errors won't necessarily be penalised.
- **Full range of tenses**: The handbook notes that candidates should aim to use a range of tenses, expressions, and vocabulary, even if some minor mistakes occur.
- **Consistent variety of English**: Don't switch between British and American spelling within the same response.
- **Beware of overuse**: Using the same word repeatedly (*laugh, funny, friend*) drags down the Language mark — the Band 3 sample (Candidate D) lost marks for this.
