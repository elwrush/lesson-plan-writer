## Paper 2: Writing — Overview

| | Part | Task | Words | Marks |
|---|------|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Compulsory | Email | ~100 | 20 |
| 2 | Choice | Article **or** Story | ~100 | 20 |

**Total: 45 minutes, 40 marks** (4 subscales × 5 bands × 2 tasks)

Four subscales: **Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, Language** — each worth 25%.

---

## Writing Part 1 — Compulsory (Email)

**Task:** Candidates deal with input material of 100–120 words: a standard rubric, an email, and four prompts presented as notes linked to parts of the email. Must include all four content points in a response of ~100 words.

**Functions tested:** Agreeing, disagreeing, giving an opinion, offering, explaining.

**How to approach:**
- Be clear about who they are writing to, why, and what to include
- Read the email carefully and answer all four prompts
- **Do not** learn a pre-prepared answer
- Practise planning carefully before writing
- Practise writing timed answers within the word length
- Evaluate own and others' answers against the criteria
- After writing, read through to check meaning and content points

---

## Writing Part 2 — Choice (Article or Story)

**Task:** Choose either an article or a story. Write ~100 words.

- **Article:** Read an announcement from a magazine or website. Gives the topic and possibly questions to respond to.
- **Story:** Given the first sentence. Must continue with clear links to the opening.

**How to approach:**
- Plan carefully before writing
- Practise timed answers
- Choose the task that best suits their interests and language ability
- For stories: pay close attention to names/pronouns in the opening sentence and maintain consistency

## Assessment Criteria — B1 Band 5 Descriptors

| Criterion | Band 5 |
|-----------|--------|
| **Content** | All content is relevant to the task. Target reader is fully informed. |
| **Communicative Achievement** | Uses the conventions of the communicative task to hold the target reader's attention and communicate straightforward ideas. |
| **Organisation** | Text is generally well organised and coherent, using a variety of linking words and cohesive devices. |
| **Language** | Uses a range of everyday vocabulary appropriately, with occasional inappropriate use of less common lexis. Uses a range of simple and some complex grammatical forms with a good degree of control. Errors do not impede communication. |

### Key points from the handbook

**Content:** "Relevant" means related to required content points/task requirements. "Target reader is informed" if content points are addressed and appropriately developed.

**Communicative Achievement:** Conventions include genre, format, register, function. "Holding attention" means the text allows a reader to derive meaning without being distracted. Straightforward ideas = concrete, limited subject matter.

**Organisation:** 
- **Linking words** = higher-frequency items (and, but, because, first of all, finally)
- **Cohesive devices** = more sophisticated (moreover, as a result) + grammatical devices (reference pronouns, substitution, ellipsis, repetition)
- **Organisational patterns** = less explicit ways (climactic order, parallelism, rhetorical questions)

**Language:**
- **Everyday vocabulary** = common, non-technical situations
- **Less common lexis** = words that appear less often, help express ideas more precisely
- **Simple grammatical forms** = words, phrases, basic tenses, simple clauses
- **Complex grammatical forms** = noun clauses, relative/adverb clauses, subordination, passive forms, infinitives, verb patterns, modal forms, tense contrasts
- **Non-impeding errors** are not necessarily penalised
- Candidates should aim to show the **full range** of their language ability and be **ambitious**

### Band 3 minimum pass

| Criterion | Band 3 |
|-----------|--------|
| Content | Minor irrelevances/omissions may be present. Target reader is on the whole informed. |
| Comm. Achievement | Uses conventions in generally appropriate ways to communicate straightforward ideas. |
| Organisation | Text is connected and coherent, using basic linking words and a limited number of cohesive devices. |
| Language | Uses everyday vocabulary generally appropriately, occasionally overusing certain lexis. Uses simple grammatical forms with a good degree of control. Errors are noticeable, but meaning can still be determined. |

## Sample Article — Band 5 (Candidate C)

**Prompt:** "What makes you laugh? Write an article telling us what you find funny and who you enjoy laughing with. Do you think it's good to laugh a lot? Why?"

**Response:**
> I love to watch comedies a lot because it makes me laugh. The comedy I love the most is the Chinese Running Man. I enjoy watching and laughing it with my family. In the show, famous actors and actresses must overcome some challenging quests, such as trading a coffee bean with someone else for something more expensive and racing in the mud to capture the flag. The storylines are very interesting and they always tickles my funny bone. Laughing out loud is great! Laughing can help us to release stress and make us feel better. It may also make us more attractive too!

**Examiner comments:**
- **Content 5** — Fully informed about what they find funny, who they laugh with. Final point developed well.
- **Comm. Achievement 5** — Conventions of article writing used (sharing personal info, expressing opinions). Holds reader's attention. Clear introduction. Nice balance between personal feeling and wider effects.
- **Organisation 5** — Generally well organised and coherent. Variety of linking words and cohesive devices (reference to avoid repetition: because it; The comedy I love the most; it; In the show; such as; and they).
- **Language 5** — Range of everyday vocab + less common lexis (comedies, challenging quests, trading, capture the flag, tickles my funny bone, release stress, attractive). Range of simple + complex forms (modal verbs: must overcome, can help, may make). Errors do not impede.

## Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Target reader** | The hypothetical reader set up in the task (e.g., a magazine's readership, your English teacher) |
| **Conventions of the task** | Genre, format, register, and function appropriate to the task type |
| **Register** | The tone of writing — formal or informal — must be appropriate for the task and target reader |
| **Linking words** | Higher-frequency items providing explicit linkage (and, but, because, first of all, finally) |
| **Cohesive devices** | More sophisticated linking (moreover, as a result) + grammatical devices (reference pronouns, substitution, ellipsis, repetition) |
| **Simple grammatical forms** | Words, phrases, basic tenses, simple clauses |
| **Complex grammatical forms** | Noun clauses, relative/adverb clauses, subordination, passive forms, infinitives, verb patterns, modal forms, tense contrasts |
| **Non-impeding errors** | Errors that do not affect communication — not necessarily penalised |
| **Overuse** | Repeatedly using the same word due to lack of resources to vary it |

## Tips for Teachers (from the handbook)

1. Learners should be very familiar with the writing tasks and their requirements **before** the exam.
2. For articles: learners should plan and write short article-like texts regularly on subjects that interest them.
3. Learners should **read articles** in magazines and websites to identify how they are organised.
4. Practise evaluating own and others' answers against the assessment criteria.
5. Encourage learners to show the **full range** of their language ability and be ambitious.
6. Non-impeding errors (spelling, grammar, punctuation that don't affect communication) are not necessarily penalised.
7. Responses that are too short may not show adequate range; responses too long may contain irrelevant content.
8. Use a consistent variety of English (don't switch between British and American spelling).
9. Aim to use a **range of tenses, expressions, and vocabulary** even if some minor mistakes occur.
